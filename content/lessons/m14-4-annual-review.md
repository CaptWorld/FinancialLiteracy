:::hook
Good financial lives aren't built by daily attention. They're built by a few good systems and **one honest hour a year**. Most people never have that hour, and small problems (a missing nominee, a lapsed policy, an old salary account, a drifting allocation) quietly grow.
:::

## The one-hour annual review

Pick a month (April works well: new financial year, appraisal season) and go through:

| Area | Check | Module |
|---|---|---|
| **Net worth** | Update assets and liabilities; compare with last year | 1 |
| **Statements** | Charges, auto-debits, unknown transactions | 0 |
| **Emergency fund** | Still 3–6+ months of current expenses? | 13 |
| **Debt** | Any high-cost debt? Prepayment plan on track? | 4 |
| **Insurance** | Health cover enough? Term cover still matches needs? | 5 |
| **Goals** | Progress vs plan; dates or amounts to change? | 13 |
| **Allocation** | Within your band? Rebalance with new money first | 14 |
| **SIPs** | Step up with your raise | 7 |
| **Taxes** | Regime for this year; harvesting gains done? | 12 |
| **Credit report** | Free report from each bureau; errors? | 3 |
| **Nominees & family file** | Every account and policy; file up to date | 5, 16 |

## Judging funds fairly

- Compare with the fund's **benchmark index** and **category**, over **5+ years**.
- For index funds, check **tracking error** and **expense ratio**, not returns vs other categories.
- Ask: did the **strategy, manager or costs** change?

**Good reasons to switch:** persistent underperformance against the benchmark, a strategy or manager change you don't trust, a much cheaper equivalent, or the fund no longer fits the goal.

**Bad reasons:** one bad year, another category did better, a friend's fund did better, a news headline.

## Life events: review again

Marriage, a child, a job change or loss, buying a home, parents' health changes, an inheritance or bonus: each changes insurance needs, goals, nominees and sometimes taxes. Do a mini-review within a month.

## When to pay for help

A **fee-only SEBI-registered investment adviser (RIA)** charges you directly, can't take commissions on products, and must act in your interest. Worth considering for:

- A large inheritance, bonus or property sale
- ESOPs/RSUs, foreign assets, early retirement planning (Module 16)
- Untangling many old insurance policies and funds (Module 17)
- When decisions feel too big or too emotional to make alone

For taxes with business income, F&O, foreign assets or notices, a **CA** is the right professional.

:::trap
"Free advice" from a bank RM, insurance agent or app is usually paid for by commissions on what you buy. That isn't always bad advice, but it's never neutral. Ask anyone advising you: **"How are you paid for this recommendation?"**
:::
