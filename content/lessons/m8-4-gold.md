:::hook
Indian households hold one of the world's largest stocks of private gold, mostly as jewellery. Families often call it "our investment". When it's actually sold, the making charges, GST and purity deductions mean it rarely fetches what the family thinks it's worth.
:::

## What gold does

- **No income**: no interest, no dividends. You earn only if someone pays more later.
- **Rupee hedge**: in India, gold price ≈ global $ price × ₹/$. When the rupee weakens, Indian gold rises even if world gold doesn't (Module 6).
- **Crisis behaviour**: often holds up or rises in panics, though not always.
- **Long flat stretches**: gold has gone years with little or no gain. It's a **diversifier**, not a growth engine like equity.

Many planners suggest a modest share (often cited as 5–10% of a portfolio) if you want gold at all. That's a rule of thumb, not a requirement.

## The forms of gold, compared

| Form | Regulated? | Costs | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jewellery** | BIS hallmarking for purity | Making charges + 3% GST, deductions on sale | Consumption; check the HUID |
| **Coins / bars** | BIS hallmark | Premium over gold price + GST | Storage and safety are your problem |
| **Gold ETF** | SEBI | Low expense ratio; brokerage | Needs a demat account; trades like a share |
| **Gold mutual fund (fund of funds)** | SEBI | ETF cost + a bit more | No demat needed; SIP possible |
| **Sovereign Gold Bonds** (existing) | Government of India | Bought at market price on exchange | Pay a small fixed interest; **no new issues since Feb 2024** |
| **"Digital gold" apps** | **Not regulated by SEBI or RBI** | Spreads and storage charges | SEBI cautioned investors in Nov 2025 |

:::example
Arjun's mother buys a chain: gold worth ₹1,00,000, making charges 12%, GST 3%.

- She pays: 1,00,000 × 1.12 × 1.03 ≈ **₹1,15,360**
- Selling back soon after, a jeweller might offer roughly the gold value minus a few % ≈ **₹97,000**

Gold has to rise about **19%** just for her to break even. That's fine for jewellery bought to wear and pass down. It isn't an investment.
:::

## Silver

Silver behaves like a more volatile cousin of gold, with heavy industrial demand (solar panels, electronics). **Silver ETFs and funds** (SEBI-regulated) exist since 2022. Expect much bigger swings than gold.

:::trap
"Gold savings schemes" from jewellers (pay monthly for 11 months, get a bonus month) are not deposits and aren't regulated like them. You're tied to that jeweller's prices and making charges, and some have collapsed with customers' money. Prefer a gold fund SIP if you want to accumulate gold.
:::

:::dive Why SGBs were popular, and why they stopped
Sovereign Gold Bonds paid the gold price at maturity **plus** a fixed yearly interest, and gains at maturity were tax-free. That made them the best way to hold gold for eight years. But as gold prices soared, the government's liability grew sharply, and it stopped new issues after February 2024. Existing SGBs still trade on exchanges, sometimes at a discount or premium to gold; the interest and tax rules attached to the original bond continue.
:::
