:::hook
Most investors don't lose money because they picked the wrong fund. They lose it by buying after a rally, selling after a crash, chasing tips, and holding losers out of pride. **Your brain is wired for survival, not for investing.** Knowing its tricks is the cheapest edge you'll ever get.
:::

## The biases and what they cost

| Bias | What it feels like | Typical money mistake | Counter-rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loss aversion** | "I can't bear to see it down 20%" | Panic selling in crashes; holding losers | Allocation you can hold; no daily checking |
| **Recency** | "This fund gave 45% last year!" | Chasing last year's winners | Choose by category fit and cost |
| **Herding / FOMO** | "Everyone's making money in IPOs/crypto" | Buying at peaks | 30-day cooling-off for new ideas |
| **Overconfidence** | "I've cracked it" | Trading, concentration, leverage | Written limits; compare with an index yearly |
| **Anchoring** | "I'll sell when it's back to my price" | Holding broken investments | "Would I buy it today?" test |
| **Mental accounting** | "Bonus is fun money"; "FDs are untouchable" | Spending windfalls; paying 40% card interest while holding FDs | Look at total net worth and total interest |
| **Sunk cost** | "I've paid 7 years of premiums, can't stop now" | Continuing bad policies | Judge only future costs and benefits (Module 5) |
| **Confirmation bias** | Only reading views that agree | Ignoring warning signs | Write the "what would prove me wrong" line |

:::example
Arjun's colleague, 2021: small-caps had doubled, so he moved his SIPs from an index fund to a small-cap fund (**recency, herding**). In the next sharp fall he stopped the SIP (**loss aversion**). He restarted only after a strong rally (**recency again**). Same market, same funds as the index investor, and a much worse result, purely from behaviour.
:::

## Rules that protect you from yourself

1. **Automate** saving and investing (Module 13).
2. **Write an investment policy** of one page: goals, allocation, rebalancing rule, what you'll never do.
3. **Cooling-off**: 30 days before any new product, theme or large purchase.
4. **Check less**: quarterly is plenty for long-term money.
5. **Compare with a benchmark**, not with friends or social media screenshots.
6. **Pre-commit in calm times**: decide now what you'll do in a 30% crash ("continue SIPs, rebalance").

:::trap
Social media shows survivors and bragging, never the full record. The colleague posting today's 300% gain rarely posts the 80% loss next year. Comparing yourself with highlight reels is a reliable way to take bad risks.
:::

:::dive Why "doing nothing" is so hard and so profitable
Humans feel safer acting. In investing, though, most actions (switching, timing, trading) add costs and errors. Studies of investor behaviour, including SEBI's data on traders, consistently show that more activity tends to mean worse results for individuals. A plan that asks you to do very little, and to do it reliably, is a feature, not laziness.
:::
