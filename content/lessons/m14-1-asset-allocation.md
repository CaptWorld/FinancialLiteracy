:::hook
People spend hours choosing between two similar funds and minutes deciding how much to put in equity at all. **The second decision drives most of what happens to your money**: how much it can grow, how far it can fall, and whether you'll panic.
:::

## The main asset classes

| Asset | Role | Bad case to expect |
|---|---|---|
| **Equity** (index/equity funds, shares) | Long-term growth above inflation | Falls of 30–60% in severe crashes; years to recover |
| **Debt** (EPF, PPF, FDs, debt funds, bonds) | Stability, near-term goals | Low real returns; rate and credit risk in some debt funds |
| **Gold** (optional) | Diversifier, rupee hedge | Long flat periods |
| **Cash** (savings, liquid) | Emergencies, short-term | Loses to inflation over time |

## Two kinds of risk you must respect

- **Risk capacity**: can your **finances** survive a fall? It depends on job stability, dependants, emergency fund, and when you need the money.
- **Risk tolerance**: can your **nerves** survive it? Many people discover in their first crash that they're far less tolerant than they thought.

**Plan for the lower of the two.** An allocation you abandon in a crash is worse than a more modest one you keep.

## Allocate per goal

:::example
Arjun's portfolio today:

| Holding | ₹ | Type |
|---|---|---|
| EPF | 3,00,000 | Debt |
| FD / emergency fund | 1,00,000 | Debt/cash |
| Index fund (retirement) | 2,00,000 | Equity |
| **Total** | **6,00,000** | **Equity 33% · Debt 67%** |

For **retirement (30+ years)** he could hold much more equity. For the **bike (2 years)** and **emergency fund**, none. His overall split comes from adding up each goal's sensible mix.
:::

A common starting point for **long-term** money for someone in their 20s–30s is a large equity share (often 60–80%), with the rest in debt, *if* their capacity and tolerance allow. There's no single right number. Old rules like "100 minus your age in equity" are rough guides, not answers.

## The glide path

As a goal approaches, shift its money from equity to safe options, say over the last **2–3 years**. That way a crash just before the goal doesn't wreck it.

:::trap
Counting only mutual funds and shares when judging your allocation. **EPF, PPF, FDs and even the emergency fund are part of the picture.** Many young salaried people are far more conservative overall than they realise, and some who think they're "diversified" own five equity funds and nothing else.
:::

:::dive Why simple portfolios work
A broad equity index fund + EPF/PPF + an FD or debt fund covers almost everything a salaried person needs. More funds rarely add diversification (Module 7, Lesson 5: overlap); they add complexity and decisions to get wrong. Professionals often spend most of their effort on allocation and costs, not on picking the next hot fund.
:::
