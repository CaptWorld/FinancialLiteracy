:::hook
"Earn 12–14% fixed returns!" ads for bonds, P2P platforms and invoice-discounting apps fill Instagram. Some are legitimate products with real risks; some are disasters waiting to happen. **The return tells you how much risk you're taking. The ad won't.**
:::

## Government bonds and T-bills, directly

Through **RBI Retail Direct**, you can open a free account with RBI and buy:

- **G-secs**: government bonds of different maturities, paying interest twice a year
- **Treasury bills**: short-term (91/182/364 days), sold at a discount
- **State Development Loans**: state government bonds

They're the safest rupee assets, with no default risk. But **prices move with interest rates** if you sell before maturity (Module 6, Lesson 5), and interest is taxable.

## Corporate bonds and credit ratings

Companies pay more than the government because they might not repay. **Credit rating agencies** (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings and others) grade issuers:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AAA | Highest safety |
| AA / A | High to adequate safety |
| BBB | Moderate, the lowest "investment grade" |
| BB and below | Speculative |
| D | In default |

Ratings are **opinions**. Several highly rated companies have collapsed in India (IL&FS in 2018 was AAA months before defaulting). Spread money across issuers and prefer high ratings.

**Online bond platforms** must be **SEBI-registered**. Check: the issuer, the rating, secured or unsecured, how easily you can sell before maturity, and what happens in default.

## REITs and InvITs

- **REITs** own completed, rent-earning property like office parks and malls.
- **InvITs** own infrastructure like toll roads, power transmission lines and gas pipelines.
- Both trade on stock exchanges, and must distribute most of their cash flows to unitholders, so they pay regular income (partly interest, partly dividend, partly return of capital, each taxed differently).
- Unit prices still move like shares. They're **not** fixed income.

They let you own a slice of commercial property or infrastructure with small amounts. They're a niche add-on, not a core holding for most beginners.

## The risky edges

:::trap
**P2P lending** platforms are RBI-registered NBFCs, but your money is lent to individual borrowers. RBI tightened rules in 2024: platforms can't promise assured returns or guaranteed early exits. Expect some defaults. Only spare money, spread thinly.

**Crypto** is not legal tender in India. Gains are taxed at a flat high rate, losses can't be set off against other income, and TDS applies on transfers. Prices can fall 70–80%. If you hold any, only an amount you could lose completely without it affecting your goals.

**Unlisted / pre-IPO shares**, **invoice discounting**, **"fractional real estate"**: varying regulation, low liquidity, complex risks. Not for beginners.
:::

:::dive Ladders: a calm way to hold fixed income
Instead of one big 5-year FD, split it into five FDs maturing in years 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5. Each year one matures: use it if needed, or reinvest it for 5 years. You always have some money becoming available, and you average out interest rates instead of betting on one moment. The same works with G-secs or target-maturity debt funds. It's a simple, robust structure for parents' retirement income too.
:::
