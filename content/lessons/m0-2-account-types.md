:::hook
Many people have three or four bank accounts and couldn't tell you which type each one is or what it charges. **That's how ₹500 here and ₹300 there disappears every quarter.**
:::

## The five accounts

| Account | What it's for | Interest | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Savings** | Everyday money, emergencies | Low | Any time |
| **Salary** | Savings account where your salary lands | Same as savings | Any time |
| **Current** | Businesses | None | Any time |
| **FD** (Fixed Deposit) | A lump sum you won't need for a set time | Higher, fixed | At maturity (penalty if early) |
| **RD** (Recurring Deposit) | Saving a fixed amount every month | Like an FD | At maturity |

## Savings account

- Your **default** account. UPI, debit card and salary all run through it.
- Interest is calculated on your **daily balance** and usually credited every quarter. Rates differ a lot between banks, so check yours.
- **Minimum average balance (MAB)**: many accounts require you to keep an average amount. If you fall short, you're charged a fee.
- Every bank must offer a **BSBDA** (Basic Savings Bank Deposit Account). It's zero-balance, with limits on the number of withdrawals.

:::dive How your savings interest is actually calculated
Banks take your **closing balance every day**, work out a day's interest on it, add these up, and credit the total every quarter (some banks pay monthly).

**Day's interest = balance × rate ÷ 365**

Arjun keeps ₹40,000 for 60 days, then rent day drops it to ₹20,000 for the next 30 days. At 3% p.a.:

- 40,000 × 0.03 × 60 ÷ 365 = ₹197
- 20,000 × 0.03 × 30 ÷ 365 = ₹49
- Quarter's interest ≈ **₹246**

₹246 on ₹30,000–40,000 is why a savings account is a **parking spot, not an investment**. Some banks pay a higher rate above a balance slab (e.g. above ₹10 lakh). Check your bank's "interest rates" page.
:::

## Salary account

Your employer opens it. It's usually **zero-balance only while your salary keeps coming in**. When you change jobs, it may become a normal savings account with charges. Close it or move your new salary there.

## Current account

For shops and businesses that make many transactions. It **pays no interest**. As a salaried person, you don't need one.

## FD (Fixed Deposit)

- You lock a **lump sum** (say ₹50,000) for a **fixed period** (7 days to 10 years) at a **fixed rate**.
- **Cumulative FD**: interest is added back and paid with the principal at the end. **Non-cumulative FD**: interest is paid out monthly or quarterly.
- **Breaking early**: you usually get a lower rate than promised. Some "tax-saver" FDs can't be broken at all.
- Interest is **taxable**. The bank may deduct TDS (tax deducted at source) above a yearly threshold.

:::example
₹1,00,000 in a 1-year FD at 7% p.a. comes to about ₹1,07,000 at maturity. Break it after 6 months and the bank may pay, say, 5.5% for those 6 months instead, so you'd get about ₹1,02,700.
:::

:::dive Why does an FD pay more than a savings account?
Because you give up **liquidity**, the ability to take your money out any time. The bank can lend FD money for longer (say a 3-year car loan) knowing you won't ask for it tomorrow, so it pays you more. This trade-off shows up everywhere in finance: **the longer and less flexibly you commit money, the more you're usually paid for it.** Watch for it in bonds, PPF and NPS later.
:::

## RD (Recurring Deposit)

A **fixed amount every month** (say ₹3,000) for a **fixed period** (say 12 months), at an FD-like rate. It's good for building discipline toward a known goal. Missing instalments can cost a small penalty.

## Two more words

- **KYC (Know Your Customer)**: the bank checks your identity and address before opening an account. It's legally required. Re-KYC happens every few years. Do it only in the bank's official app or branch, never through a link someone sends you.
- **Nominee**: the person who can claim the money if something happens to you. Add one to every account and FD.

:::trap
"Zero balance" accounts that aren't. Check your last three statements for "MAB charges", "non-maintenance charges", SMS-alert fees and debit card annual fees.
:::

:::checklive dicgc
Savings, current, FD and RD balances in one bank are added together for deposit insurance. Check the current insured limit.
:::

:::task
1. In your bank app, find your account type: Savings, Salary or something else. Note it in **Me → Notes** (no account numbers).
2. Find out whether it has a minimum balance. Look in the app's account details or the bank's "schedule of charges" page.
3. Check whether a nominee is added. If not, add one. It takes two minutes in most apps.
4. Look at the last 3 months' statements for any charges.
:::
