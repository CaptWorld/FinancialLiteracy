:::hook
At 27, retirement feels like someone else's problem. But it's the goal where **time does the heaviest lifting**, and the one with no loans, no scholarships and no second chances. The maths is sobering, but not hopeless.
:::

## Step 1: what will you spend?

Start with today's **essential** monthly expenses and grow them with inflation:

:::example
Arjun: ₹48,000 a month today. Retiring at 60 (33 years), inflation 6%:

- Monthly at 60: 48,000 × 1.06³³ ≈ **₹3.28 lakh**
- Yearly: ≈ **₹39.4 lakh**

In today's rupees that's still ₹48,000 a month; prices are just higher. Healthcare and rent may rise faster, travel and commuting may fall. It's a starting estimate.
:::

## Step 2: the corpus

**Corpus ≈ yearly expenses at retirement ÷ withdrawal rate**

For a retirement of 25–35 years, use a **cautious 3–3.5%** (Module 13, Lesson 4 explains why).

- At 3.5%: 39.4 lakh ÷ 0.035 ≈ **₹11.3 crore**
- In today's rupees: 11.3 ÷ 6.84 ≈ **₹1.65 crore**

## Step 3: what EPF (and PPF/NPS) will cover

:::example
Arjun's EPF: ₹3 lakh today, about ₹7,000 a month going in (his + employer's EPF share), rising 7% a year with salary, earning ~8% (assumed):

- Projected at 60: **≈ ₹3.5 crore**

Many salaried people don't realise how much EPF builds over a full career, provided they **never withdraw it on job changes** (Module 2).
:::

## Step 4: close the gap

Gap ≈ 11.3 − 3.5 = **₹7.8 crore**. At an assumed 10% a year from a diversified equity index fund (not guaranteed):

| Approach | Monthly SIP |
|---|---|
| Flat SIP for 33 years | ≈ ₹25,000 |
| **SIP rising 10% a year** | **≈ ₹8,200 to start** |

Starting at 35 instead of 27 roughly doubles what's needed. **Time is the cheapest ingredient.** Try your numbers in **Tools → Goal planner** and **Tools → SIP & step-up**.

## The big risks

- **Healthcare**: hold a personal health policy continuously from young (Module 5), plus a medical buffer.
- **Longevity**: plan to age 90+.
- **Sequence risk**: keep 2–3 years of expenses in safe assets near and in retirement (Module 13).
- **Dipping into retirement money** for other goals, especially EPF withdrawals between jobs.

:::trap
"Retirement plans" sold by insurers often combine low returns with long lock-ins and annuities. Build retirement with EPF, PPF/NPS and low-cost index funds, and buy pure insurance separately (Module 5).
:::

:::dive Early retirement (FIRE) in India
Retiring at 40–45 means 45+ years of withdrawals, so people pursuing "FIRE" (Financial Independence, Retire Early) typically use 2.5–3% withdrawal rates, i.e. a corpus of 33–40 times yearly expenses, and very high savings rates. It's possible for some, but healthcare costs, children's education and parents' care make Indian FIRE plans need extra buffers. Treat online FIRE calculators as rough sketches, not plans.
:::

2. Download your EPF passbook and note the balance; estimate monthly contributions.
3. Work out your gap and starting SIP with the tools. Write the number down even if you can't start it yet.
:::
