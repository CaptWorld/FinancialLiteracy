## How NPS works

- You open an account (online or via your employer), get a **PRAN** (permanent retirement account number), and pick a **pension fund manager**.
- Money is invested across four asset classes:
  - **E**: equity (shares)
  - **C**: corporate bonds
  - **G**: government bonds
  - **A**: alternatives (a small capped slice)
- **Auto choice**: a lifecycle mix that starts with more equity and shifts to bonds as you age. **Active choice**: you choose the split, within PFRDA's limits (the equity cap falls with age).
- **Costs are very low** compared with most mutual funds and insurance products.

## Tier I and Tier II

| | Tier I | Tier II |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Pension | Optional savings add-on |
| Withdrawals | Restricted until retirement (some partial withdrawals for specific needs) | Flexible |
| Tax benefits | Yes (rules vary by regime) | Generally none |

## At exit: lump sum and annuity

At retirement (or normal exit), part of the corpus can be taken as a **lump sum** and the rest must buy an **annuity**: a pension from a life insurer, paid for life. Annuity income is **taxable** and annuity rates are modest.

PFRDA **overhauled the exit and withdrawal rules in December 2025** for non-government subscribers: the lump-sum share, the small-corpus limits and the exit timing all changed. Rules like these can change again before you retire, so check them on PFRDA's site rather than relying on old articles or this course.

## The tax angle

- **Employer contributions** to your NPS (the corporate model, through your CTC) get favourable tax treatment, often under **both** tax regimes, within limits.
- **Your own contributions** get deductions mainly under the **old** regime.
- Part of the lump sum at exit is tax-free; annuity income is taxed at your slab.

Since April 2026 these rules sit in the new Income-tax Act, 2025, with new section numbers. Module 12 covers them; check the Income Tax Department's site for current limits.

## Who it suits

**Good fit:** you want a cheap, hands-off retirement account; your employer offers NPS tax-efficiently; you value being *unable* to dip into retirement money.

**Poor fit:** you may need the money before retirement; you dislike the compulsory annuity; you'd rather manage an EPF + PPF + index fund mix with full flexibility.

:::example

:::trap
"Invest in NPS just for tax saving" without checking your regime. Under the new regime, your own NPS contributions may give no deduction at all, leaving you with only the lock-in. Run the numbers for **your** regime first.
:::
