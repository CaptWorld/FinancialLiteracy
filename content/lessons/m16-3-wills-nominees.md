:::hook
When a family member dies, grief is followed by paperwork: accounts with no nominee, a demat account nobody knew about, a policy whose nominee had died years earlier, siblings disagreeing. **Much of this is prevented by an hour of work today.**
:::

## Nominee vs legal heir

- **Nominee**: the person an institution pays, so the claim is quick.
- **Legal heir**: who actually **owns** the money, decided by your **will**, or by **succession law** if there's no will.

For **bank accounts, shares, mutual funds and most investments**, the nominee generally receives the money **as a trustee for the legal heirs**. The Supreme Court confirmed this for shares in 2023: nomination doesn't override succession.

**Exception: life insurance.** If the nominee is your **spouse, children or parents**, they're **beneficial nominees** and receive the claim money outright.

## Multiple nominees: the new rules

- **Bank accounts and deposits**: up to **4 nominees** from **1 November 2025**, with set percentages (simultaneous) or one after another (successive). Lockers and safe-custody items follow successive nomination.
- **Mutual funds and demat accounts**: up to **10 nominees**, with percentages (SEBI's revised rules).
- **EPF**: nominate through the UAN portal (e-nomination).
- **Insurance**: nominate on every policy; update after marriage or a death in the family.

## Wills, simply

- A will states who inherits what. It must be **written**, **signed by you**, and **witnessed by two people** (who shouldn't be beneficiaries).
- **Registration** is optional but adds credibility.
- Update it after marriage, children, a large purchase, or a beneficiary's death.
- Name an **executor** you trust.
- Without a will, assets pass under the **personal succession law** that applies to you (based on religion), which may not match your wishes and often needs court certificates.

:::example
Arjun, 27, single: he names his mother as nominee on his bank, EPF, funds and term policy. He writes a one-page will leaving his assets to his mother, and to his sister if his mother isn't alive, signed in front of two friends. He tells his sister where the will and his family file are. Total time: about an hour.
:::

## The family file

One document (paper or a shared, secure note) listing:

- Bank accounts, FDs, EPF/PPF/NPS, mutual funds (CAS), demat, insurance policies: **institution and type only, plus where documents are**
- Nominees for each
- Where the will is kept, and the executor
- Loans and EMIs
- Whom to contact (CA, adviser, HR)

**Don't** put passwords, PINs or full account numbers in it. Store those separately and securely.

:::trap
Joint accounts and nominees aren't a substitute for a will, and "my brother will sort it out" isn't a plan. Disputes are common even in close families when nothing is written down.
:::

:::dive Help parents with theirs
Parents often hold FDs, policies and property with old or missing nominees, and no will. Offering to help them list everything and update nominees (Module 17) is one of the most valuable things you can do for your family's finances, and a natural way to start money conversations.
:::

:::task
1. Check nominees on every account and policy you hold. Add or update them; consider adding a second nominee where allowed.
2. Draft a simple will (even handwritten) and get it witnessed. Note where it's kept.
3. Start your family file and tell one trusted person where it is.
:::
