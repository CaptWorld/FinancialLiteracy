## The six jobs money does

| Job | Plain meaning | Where it's taught |
|---|---|---|
| **Earn** | Salary, freelance, rent, interest | Module 2 |
| **Spend** | Rent, food, EMIs, fun | Module 1 (budget) |
| **Save** | Money kept safe for near needs and emergencies | Module 0, 1 |
| **Invest** | Money put to work to grow faster than prices rise | Modules 7, 8, 10 |
| **Protect** | Insurance so one bad event doesn't wipe you out | Module 5 |
| **Borrow** | Using someone else's money now, paying more later | Modules 3–4 |

Almost every money mistake is one of these done in the wrong order. A common one is investing before having an emergency fund or insurance.

Around all six sits **the economy**: inflation, interest rates set by RBI, jobs, the rupee. It decides how much your FD pays and your EMI costs. Module 6 explains it.

## What a bank does

1. You **deposit** money. You are effectively lending it to the bank.
2. The bank **lends** most of it to others: home loans, car loans, business loans.
3. Borrowers pay the bank **more interest** than the bank pays you. That gap, plus fees, is the bank's income.

:::example
You keep ₹1,00,000 in a fixed deposit (FD) at 7% p.a. and earn about ₹7,000 in a year. The bank lends that money as a personal loan at, say, 12% p.a. and earns about ₹12,000. The ₹5,000 difference pays the bank's staff, branches, bad loans and profit.
:::

## Interest: the price of money

- **You earn interest** on savings accounts, FDs and RDs, because the bank is using your money.
- **You pay interest** on loans and unpaid credit card bills, because you're using the bank's money.
- Rates are almost always quoted **per year ("p.a.")**. A rate like "2% per month" sounds small, but it's about 24% a year, or more after compounding (Module 1).

:::dive How banks "create" money, and why RBI keeps a leash on them
When a bank gives Arjun a ₹5 lakh car loan, it doesn't take ₹5 lakh out of someone's locker. It simply **credits ₹5 lakh to the car dealer's account**. New deposit money has appeared. Most money in India today exists as bank deposits created this way, not as notes.

That's powerful and risky. If a bank lends too much to people who can't repay, it can run out of money when depositors want theirs back. So RBI sets guard-rails:

- **CRR (Cash Reserve Ratio)**: a share of deposits the bank must park with RBI, earning nothing.
- **SLR (Statutory Liquidity Ratio)**: a share it must hold in very safe, easy-to-sell assets, mostly government bonds.
- **Capital rules**: the bank's owners must have their own money at stake, so losses hit them before depositors.

RBI also changes the **repo rate**, the rate at which banks borrow from RBI. When it goes up, loans and FDs usually get dearer and pay more; when it goes down, they get cheaper and pay less. Module 6 covers this properly. The current CRR, SLR and repo rate change from time to time, so check them on RBI's site.
:::

## Who keeps banks honest

- **RBI** (Reserve Bank of India) licenses and supervises banks, sets the rules on charges and complaints, and handles complaints banks don't fix.
- **DICGC**, part of RBI, insures your bank deposits up to a limit if a bank fails (Module 3).
- Kinds of banks you'll see: public sector (SBI), private (HDFC, ICICI), small finance banks, and **payments banks** (e.g. Airtel Payments Bank). Payments banks can hold money and make payments, but they can't lend.

:::trap
"Bank-like" schemes that are not banks: chit funds run informally, "investment clubs", apps promising fixed monthly returns. They have no deposit insurance. Check an entity on RBI's **Sachet** portal before giving anyone money.
:::

:::checklive sachet
Search an entity's name to see whether it's allowed to take deposits, or report one that isn't.
:::

## Indian number words

- 1 lakh = 1,00,000
- 10 lakh = 10,00,000 = 1 million
- 1 crore = 100 lakh = 1,00,00,000 = 10 million
