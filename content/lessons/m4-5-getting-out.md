## Prepaying: why early matters

In Lesson 1 you saw that early EMIs are mostly interest. A lump sum paid early cuts the principal that all future interest is calculated on.

:::example
Arjun's ₹40 lakh home loan at 8.5%, EMI ₹34,713. At the end of year 2 he prepays **₹1 lakh** and keeps the EMI unchanged.

- Loan ends about **13 months earlier**
- Interest saved ≈ **₹3.4 lakh**

Try it in **Tools → EMI** (prepayment section) with your own loan.
:::

**Charges:** for floating-rate loans taken by **individuals for non-business purposes**, lenders can't charge prepayment or foreclosure penalties, and there's no minimum lock-in. Fixed-rate loans may still carry charges, so check the KFS and loan agreement before you prepay.

:::checklive rbi-notifications
RBI's 2025 directions on prepayment charges (in force for loans sanctioned or renewed from 1 January 2026) list exactly which loans are covered.
:::

## Which debt first?

1. **Stop the bleeding**: no new card spending you can't clear in full this month.
2. **Keep a small emergency buffer** (even ₹10–20k), so one surprise doesn't push you back to the card.
3. **List every debt** with balance and rate.
4. Pay minimums on all, then put every spare rupee on:
   - **Avalanche**: the highest rate first. Mathematically best.
   - **Snowball**: the smallest balance first. Quick wins keep some people going.

Either beats spreading money thin. Clearing a 42% card balance is the best guaranteed "investment" you'll ever see.

## Prepay or invest?

| Your debt | Usual answer |
|---|---|
| Card, personal loan, app loan (high rate) | **Prepay first.** No investment reliably beats it. |
| Car or bike loan (moderate) | Usually prepay, once you have an emergency fund |
| Home loan (lowest rate) | **A mix.** Prepaying gives a guaranteed saving equal to the rate; investing long-term may earn more but isn't certain. Your peace of mind matters. |

Never stop retirement contributions such as EPF to prepay a cheap loan.

## Settlement vs closure

When you can't pay, lenders sometimes offer a **one-time settlement**: you pay less than you owe. It ends the calls, but your credit report shows **"settled"**, which tells future lenders you didn't repay in full. It can block or raise the cost of loans for years. Exhaust other options first: restructuring, a longer tenure, family help, or selling an asset. If you do settle, later pay the remaining amount and ask the lender to update the status to "closed".

## After you close a loan

- Get a **No Objection Certificate (NOC)** / closure letter.
- **Original property documents**: lenders must return them within **30 days** of full repayment. Delays beyond that entitle you to compensation (₹5,000 per day).
- For vehicle loans, get the lender's **hypothecation removed** from the RC (vehicle registration).
- After 30–60 days, **check your credit report** shows the loan as closed with zero balance.

## Your rights against harassment

RBI's rules for lenders and recovery agents:

- No calls **before 8 am or after 7 pm**.
- No threats, abuse, intimidation, or calling your family, friends or colleagues to shame you.
- Agents must identify themselves and carry authorisation from the lender.
- Loan apps can't access your contacts or photos for recovery.

If this happens: complain to the lender's grievance officer in writing → after 30 days or an unsatisfactory reply, RBI's complaint portal. For threats or fake "legal notices", call 1930 or file at cybercrime.gov.in.

:::checklive rbi-cms
File a complaint against a bank or NBFC if they don't resolve your issue.
:::

:::dive Guarantor and co-borrower: what you're really signing
A **co-borrower** is equally responsible for the loan from day one; it appears on their credit report. A **guarantor** must pay if the borrower doesn't, and a default hits the guarantor's credit report too. Sign only for someone whose loan you could actually afford to repay yourself, in writing, after reading the KFS.
:::
