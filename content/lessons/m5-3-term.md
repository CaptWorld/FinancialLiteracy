:::hook
Many Indian families with "life insurance" are covered for only a few lakh rupees, often through endowment plans bought for tax saving. If the earner dies, that's a year or two of household expenses. **The right amount of term cover often costs less per year than one phone.**
:::

## What term insurance is

You pay a yearly premium for a fixed term, say until age 60. If you die during the term, your nominee receives the **sum assured**. If you don't, nothing is paid. There's no savings part, so the premium is small for a large cover.

**Do you need it?** Only if someone depends on your income (parents, spouse, children) or would inherit your debts. A single person with no dependants and no co-signed loans may not need it yet, though buying early locks in a low premium.

## How much cover?

Ask: **"If my income stopped tomorrow, what would my dependants need?"**

:::example
Arjun's needs today:

| Need | Amount |
|---|---|
| Support for parents: ₹96,000/year for ~20 years, rising 5% a year (value today, money earning 7%) | ≈ ₹15.1 lakh |
| Bike loan outstanding | ₹1.2 lakh |
| Sister's wedding commitment | ₹4 lakh |
| **Minus** savings that would pass to them | − ₹1.2 lakh |
| **Cover needed today** | **≈ ₹19 lakh** |

A rough cross-check of 10–15× yearly income gives ₹78 lakh–₹1.2 crore, because it assumes future needs like a spouse and children. Arjun could buy **₹50 lakh–₹1 crore** now, while it's cheap and he's healthy, or buy what he needs now and add another policy at marriage.
:::

**Term until when?** Until dependants no longer rely on you, usually **60–65**. Cover to 85 or 99 costs much more, and by then you should have savings instead.

## Buying it well

1. **Compare 2–3 insurers** on premium, claim settlement record, and policy wording.
2. **Disclose everything**: health, smoking (even past), alcohol, medicines, risky hobbies, other policies.
3. Do the **medical tests** the insurer asks for; they protect your claim.
4. **Add a nominee**, and tell them the policy exists, with the insurer's name. Keep a copy where family can find it.
5. Use the **30-day free-look period** to cancel if the policy isn't what you were told.

:::trap
"Return of premium" and "zero-cost" term plans charge much more to hand back your own money without growth. Riders (critical illness, accidental death) add cost. Consider them separately on their merits rather than accepting a bundle.
:::

:::dive Claim settlement ratio, and why it's not the whole story
IRDAI's annual report lists each life insurer's **claim settlement ratio**: the % of death claims paid. Higher is better, but it counts claims, not money, and big insurers with many small policies can look better than they are. Also check the **amount settlement ratio** and the time taken. Most rejections come from non-disclosure, which you control.
:::

:::dive When you marry: MWPA
A term policy bought under the **Married Women's Property Act (MWPA)** is held in trust for your wife and/or children. Creditors and other relatives can't claim it. It's worth knowing about once you marry. Ask the insurer for the MWPA option when buying.
:::
