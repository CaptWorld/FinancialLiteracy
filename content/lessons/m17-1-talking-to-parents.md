:::hook
Many Indian families never talk about money until a hospital bill, a death or a fraud forces it, at the worst possible moment. **Starting the conversation while everyone is well is one of the kindest things you can do.**
:::

## Start with care, not correction

What tends to work:

- **"If something happened, I'd want to know how to help."**
- **"I'm learning about this; can I check something with you?"**
- **"Can we make a list so nothing gets lost?"**

What tends to backfire: "Your policies are useless", "You should have invested in…", or a surprise spreadsheet. Parents have managed money for decades, often under harder conditions. Respect that.

## The family picture (built together, over a few sittings)

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| **Income** | Salary/pension, rent, interest, family support |
| **Assets** | Bank accounts and FDs (which banks), mutual funds, shares, PPF/EPF, gold, property |
| **Loans** | Home loan or others: rate, EMI, remaining tenure |
| **Insurance** | Health cover (how much, which family members), life policies (type, premium, maturity), motor/home |
| **Health** | Ongoing conditions and medicines (for insurance planning, not judgement) |
| **Paperwork** | Nominees on everything? A will? Where are documents? |

Write it down as a **family file** (Module 16, Lesson 3): institutions, types and locations, **never passwords or full numbers**.

## Order of topics

1. **Health insurance** for parents: the biggest risk to family finances (Module 5).
2. **Nominees** on every account and policy, and a **will**.
3. **Scam awareness**: seniors are prime targets (Lesson 3).
4. **Loans**: is the home-loan rate still competitive? (Module 4)
5. **Investments and policies**: review together (Lesson 2).

:::example
Arjun's first sitting with his father lasts 40 minutes. They list his father's two bank accounts, FDs, the mutual funds he manages, the home loan, the rent from a small flat, and his insurance. They find: one FD with no nominee, his mother's health cover lapsed last year, and no will. Three concrete actions, no arguments.
:::

:::trap
Taking over completely, or making changes without consent, damages trust and can create legal problems. Joint decisions, with parents in control of their own money, are both kinder and safer.
:::

:::dive Your own feelings matter too
Money talks with parents can stir up guilt (about how much you send home), worry (about their health), or old family tensions. It's normal. Keep the focus on practical, protective steps; it helps to agree in advance that nothing has to be decided in the first conversation.
:::
