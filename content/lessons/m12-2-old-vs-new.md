:::hook
Every January, offices fill with "tax-saving" sales pitches: ELSS, insurance, PPF, NPS. For many salaried people today the honest answer is: **you may not have tax to save.** For others, one regime can save tens of thousands a year. You find out only by doing the maths.
:::

## What each regime offers

| | New regime (default) | Old regime |
|---|---|---|
| Slab rates | Lower, more slabs (Lesson 1) | Higher: 5% from ₹2.5L, 20% from ₹5L, 30% above ₹10L (below age 60) |
| Standard deduction (salaried) | ₹75,000 | ₹50,000 |
| Rebate | Tax nil up to ₹12 lakh taxable | Tax nil up to ₹5 lakh taxable |
| Section 123 (old 80C): EPF, PPF, ELSS, life insurance, home-loan principal… up to ₹1.5L | ✗ | ✓ |
| Section 126 (old 80D): health insurance | ✗ | ✓ |
| HRA exemption | ✗ | ✓ |
| Home-loan interest (self-occupied) | ✗ | ✓ (up to a limit) |
| Your own NPS contributions (extra deduction) | ✗ | ✓ |
| Employer's NPS contribution | ✓ | ✓ |

Section numbers changed with the new Act; many people and websites still use the old ones (80C, 80D). Both refer to the same deductions.

:::checklive it-salaried
The Income Tax Department's page for salaried taxpayers lists current deductions and limits under each regime.
:::

## The comparison, honestly done

:::example
**Priya**, gross ₹18 lakh.

**New regime**: taxable ₹17.25 lakh → tax ≈ **₹1,50,800**.

**Old regime**, deductions she'd genuinely have:

| Deduction | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Standard deduction | 50,000 |
| Section 123 (EPF + PPF already) | 1,50,000 |
| Health insurance (self) | 25,000 |
| HRA exemption (pays high rent) | 1,80,000 |
| Own NPS contribution | 50,000 |
| **Total** | **4,55,000** |

Taxable ₹13.45 lakh → tax ≈ **₹2,24,640**. **New regime saves her ≈ ₹74,000.**

Even with a ₹2 lakh home-loan interest deduction (total ₹6.55 lakh), the old regime comes to ≈ ₹1,62,000, still more than the new. At her salary, she'd need about **₹7 lakh** of deductions for the old regime to win.
:::

## Don't buy products to "save tax"

A tax deduction only helps if:

1. you're in the **old regime**, and
2. you'd **actually pay tax** otherwise, and
3. the product is **good on its own merits**.

ELSS or PPF you'd buy anyway? Fine. An endowment plan or ULIP bought for a deduction? Usually a poor trade: you might save ₹15,000–45,000 of tax once, but lock lakhs into a low-return product for years (Module 5).

:::trap
Declaring investments to your employer in January that you never make, or fake rent receipts for HRA, is tax evasion. The department matches data through AIS, PAN and landlords' PANs, and notices with penalties can follow years later.
:::

:::dive Switching regimes
Salaried people with no business income can pick the regime **each year** when filing the return (and tell the employer at the start of the year for TDS). People with business or professional income (including F&O trading, Module 11) face stricter rules on switching back and forth. Check the department's guidance if that applies to you.
:::
