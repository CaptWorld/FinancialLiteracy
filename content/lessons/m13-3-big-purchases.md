:::hook
"Rent is money down the drain" is one of the most repeated lines in Indian families. But a home-loan EMI is mostly **interest** for the first decade, and that money doesn't come back either. **The real question isn't rent vs EMI. It's rent vs the full cost of owning.**
:::

## Rent vs buy, with real numbers

:::example
A 2BHK in Hyderabad: **₹80 lakh**. The same flat rents for **₹25,000 a month** (rental yield **3.75%**).

**Buying, upfront:**
- Down payment (20%): ₹16 lakh
- Stamp duty + registration (varies by state; ~7% assumed): ₹5.6 lakh
- Interiors, furniture: ₹5 lakh
- **Total upfront ≈ ₹26.6 lakh**

**Buying, monthly (year 1):**
- Loan ₹64 lakh at 8.5% for 20 years → **EMI ≈ ₹55,500**, of which interest ≈ ₹45,300
- Maintenance ≈ ₹3,000
- Return lost on ₹26.6 lakh upfront (if it earned 7%) ≈ ₹15,500
- **Owner's real cost ≈ ₹64,000 a month** vs **rent ₹25,000**

The flat must appreciate about **5.8% a year** just to break even with renting and investing the difference. That may happen; it may not. Some periods in Indian cities saw flat prices for years.
:::

## When buying makes sense

- You'll stay **7–10+ years** in that city.
- You value **stability**: no forced moves, schools, ageing parents.
- The EMI is comfortably within your budget (many planners suggest housing EMIs well under ~30–35% of take-home), **after** emergency fund, insurance and retirement saving.
- You've counted every upfront cost and still have a buffer.

Buying is a **lifestyle and security decision** first. Treat any price gain as a bonus, not the plan.

## Under-construction and other traps

:::trap
Under-construction flats can be delayed for years while you pay EMIs and rent at the same time. Check the project's **RERA registration**, the builder's delivery record and approvals. Avoid "subvention" schemes where the builder pays EMIs for a while: if the builder defaults, the loan is still yours.
:::

## Cars: the expensive convenience

A ₹10 lakh car can lose **15–20% of its value in the first year** and keeps losing value. Beyond the EMI you pay insurance, fuel, parking, servicing and tyres. A rule of thumb some planners use: **20% down, loan no longer than 4 years, total transport costs under ~10% of income**. In a city with good cabs and metro, compare a year of rides with a year of ownership honestly.

## A checklist for any big purchase

1. Emergency fund full? Insurance in place? Retirement SIP running?
2. Total cost (not EMI), including running costs?
3. Would you buy it if you had to pay in cash?
4. Can you wait 30 days and still want it?
5. Does it move a real goal forward, or just this month's mood?
