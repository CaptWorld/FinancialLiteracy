:::hook
For many Indian families, "safe" means a bank deposit or a debt fund. Between 2018 and 2020, depositors and debt-fund investors learned that **safe depends on who owes you the money, how much you have with them, and what exactly you bought.**
:::

## 2018: IL&FS, the AAA that defaulted

IL&FS was a huge infrastructure lending group, rated **AAA**, the highest rating. It had borrowed short-term to fund very long projects. When it couldn't roll over its debts in 2018, it **defaulted**, and ratings collapsed to default within weeks. Mutual funds holding its paper marked down their NAVs, and lending to non-bank lenders froze for months, hitting other NBFCs (DHFL defaulted in 2019).

**Lesson:** ratings are opinions; concentration in one borrower hurts; even "high-quality" debt carries credit risk (Module 8).

## 2019: PMC Bank, the cooperative bank that hid its loans

Punjab & Maharashtra Co-operative Bank had hidden large loans to a single real-estate group using thousands of dummy accounts. When this emerged, RBI restricted withdrawals, at first to very small amounts. Many depositors, including retirees with their life savings, couldn't access their money for a long time.

**What changed:** deposit insurance (DICGC) was raised from **₹1 lakh to ₹5 lakh** per depositor per bank in 2020, and RBI's oversight of cooperative banks was tightened.

**Lesson:** spread deposits across **strong banks**, keeping each within the insurance limit (Module 3). Extra interest from a weak bank isn't worth it.

## 2020: Yes Bank and its AT1 bonds

Yes Bank grew fast with risky loans. In March 2020, RBI placed it under a **moratorium**, capping withdrawals for a short period, then arranged a rescue led by SBI. Depositors were protected. But **AT1 bonds** (riskier bank bonds that can absorb losses) worth thousands of crores were **written off to zero**. Some had been sold to individuals as "FD-like".

**Lesson:** a bank's **bonds are not deposits**. They're uninsured and can be wiped out (Module 8).

## 2020: Franklin Templeton's frozen debt funds

In April 2020, as COVID panic dried up buyers, Franklin Templeton India **wound up six debt schemes** holding lower-rated, hard-to-sell bonds. Investors' money was locked and returned in instalments over the following years.

**Lesson:** debt funds chasing yield with lower-rated bonds can freeze. Keep safety money in overnight, liquid or high-quality short-term funds, or simply FDs (Module 7).

## The rules these episodes teach

1. **Higher yield = higher risk**, always (Module 0).
2. **Spread deposits** across strong banks within insurance limits.
3. **Deposits ≠ bonds ≠ debt funds**: know exactly what you hold.
4. **Ratings help but can be wrong**; don't concentrate.
5. **Emergency and near-term money in the safest, most liquid places.**
