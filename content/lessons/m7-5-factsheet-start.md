:::hook
Arjun downloads a fund's factsheet: 12 pages of tables, ratios and charts. He closes it. **About ten lines matter for a normal investor**, and you can read them in five minutes.
:::

## The factsheet: what to read

| Line | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Investment objective** | Does it match your goal? |
| **Category & benchmark** | The index it should be compared with |
| **AUM** | Not tiny (very small funds can close), not the main criterion |
| **Expense ratio (TER/BER)** | Direct plan, low for its category |
| **Exit load** | How long until you can sell free |
| **Top 10 holdings & sector mix** | Concentrated? Overlapping with funds you own? |
| **Portfolio turnover** | High = lots of trading costs |
| **Riskometer** | Matches how much fall you can handle |
| **Fund manager & tenure** | For active funds: has the person behind the record changed? |
| **Returns vs benchmark** (3, 5, 10 years) | Consistency, not last year |

The **SID (Scheme Information Document)** and **KIM (Key Information Memorandum)** are the legal documents. Skim the KIM once for any fund you buy.

## Options: growth vs IDCW

- **Growth**: gains stay invested and compound. Best for long-term goals.
- **IDCW (Income Distribution cum Capital Withdrawal)**, formerly "dividend": pays out part of **your own money** from the NAV. It's not extra income, and it's taxed at your slab. Rarely the right choice for a salaried person building wealth.

## Starting properly: the checklist

1. **KYC**: complete once through a KYC Registration Agency (KRA), via any AMC or platform, with PAN, Aadhaar and a selfie/video. It then works across all fund houses.
2. **Choose the fund** by goal (Lesson 2), cost (Lesson 3), plan (**direct**), option (**growth**).
3. **Where to buy direct**: the AMC's website/app, **MF Central** (run by the registrars CAMS and KFintech), or platforms that offer direct plans. Check the scheme name says "Direct".
4. **Nominee**: add one (or formally opt out). It saves your family huge trouble.
5. **SIP date**: a few days after salary credit, so the debit never bounces.
6. **Write down** the fund, the goal, and your rules (Lesson 4).

:::example
**Arjun's first SIP**, for retirement 30+ years away:

- Category: broad equity index fund (e.g. tracking the Nifty 50)
- Plan: Direct · Option: Growth
- Amount: ₹5,000/month, 7th of every month (salary on the 1st)
- Step-up: +10% every April
- Nominee: his mother
- Review: once a year, in April

*The specific scheme is his choice after comparing TER and tracking error across a few index funds; this course doesn't recommend individual schemes.*
:::

## Your CAS: one statement for everything

The **Consolidated Account Statement** lists all your mutual fund folios across fund houses, with units, NAV, value and transactions. If you have a demat account, the depository (NSDL/CDSL) sends one CAS covering both. It's emailed for months with transactions; you can also download it any time from MF Central or the registrars.

Check it **monthly or quarterly**: are SIPs going through? Nominees on every folio? Any scheme you didn't buy?

:::trap
Advice and selling are different jobs. A **distributor** (bank, agent, many apps selling regular plans) earns commission and must show an **AMFI ARN**. An **investment adviser** who charges you a fee must be **SEBI-registered**. Anyone else offering paid tips, on Telegram, WhatsApp or YouTube, is unregulated. Check registrations on SEBI's and AMFI's sites before paying anyone.
:::

:::dive Overlap: why five funds can be one fund
Many people own 4–6 large-cap and flexi-cap funds thinking they're diversified. Open their top-10 holdings and you'll often see the same banks, IT companies and conglomerates in each. You're paying several expense ratios for what is close to one index. One or two broad funds per need is usually enough.
:::
