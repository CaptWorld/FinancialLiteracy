:::hook
Every bubble feels different from the inside: tulips, railways, internet stocks, housing, crypto. And every crash feels like the end of the world from the inside. **History doesn't predict the next one. It teaches you how to survive any of them.**
:::

## How bubbles form

1. **A real innovation or story**: the internet, cheap housing loans, blockchain.
2. **Easy money and credit**: low rates, loose lending, leverage.
3. **Rising prices attract newcomers**, who are drawn by price, not value.
4. **"This time is different"**: old valuation rules are declared obsolete.
5. **Leverage and fraud creep in** near the top.
6. **A trigger**: rate rises, a failure, a scandal. Then the fall, often 50–90% for the hottest assets.

## Dot-com, 2000

Internet companies with little or no profit traded at sky-high values. The US tech-heavy **Nasdaq fell about 78%** from its 2000 peak by 2002. Many companies vanished. A few survivors became giants, which shows that **real innovation and terrible investments can coexist**. India's tech stocks crashed too (Module 15, Lesson 1: Ketan Parekh).

## The 2008 global financial crisis

US lenders gave home loans to borrowers who couldn't repay. These loans were bundled into complex securities, rated highly, and bought with huge leverage by banks worldwide. When house prices fell, losses spread. **Lehman Brothers collapsed** in September 2008, credit froze globally, and recession followed.

In India, the **Sensex fell about 60%** from its January 2008 peak to October 2008. Indian banks were less exposed, and the economy recovered relatively quickly. Investors who kept SIPs going bought units at deep discounts; those who sold, or were leveraged, locked in losses.

**Lessons:** leverage turns falls into wipe-outs; complex products can hide risk; ratings can fail (again).

## COVID, 2020

In March 2020 the **Nifty fell about 38%** in weeks as lockdowns began. Central banks cut rates and governments spent heavily. The Nifty **recovered to new highs by year-end**. Those who panicked and sold, and waited for "clarity", missed the rebound.

## Crypto, 2022

After a huge 2020–21 boom, rising interest rates and failures (the TerraLuna collapse, then the **FTX exchange collapse** in November 2022) sent **Bitcoin down over 75%** from its peak, with smaller tokens falling more. FTX customers' money had been misused and was lost. Many investors learned that unregulated platforms lack the protections of banks and SEBI-regulated brokers.

## What this means for you

| Lesson | Practice |
|---|---|
| Crashes are normal | Expect a 30–50% fall in equity a few times in your life |
| Leverage kills | Never borrow to invest (Modules 4, 11) |
| Forced selling locks in losses | Emergency fund + insurance first (Modules 5, 13) |
| Recoveries come without warning | Stay invested per plan; rebalance by rule (Module 14) |
| Hot assets can fall 75–90% | Keep speculative bets tiny, if any |
| Regulation matters | Use regulated products and intermediaries |

:::trap
"Buy the dip" is not a plan when the asset has no earnings or cash flows to fall back on. Broad stock indices recovered from past crashes because the underlying companies kept earning. A pure speculation can simply stay down.
:::
