:::hook
Arjun's colleague was hospitalised for dengue for five days. His company's ₹3 lakh policy "covered" it, yet he paid ₹70,000 himself. The room he picked was above the policy's room-rent limit, and that one choice cut almost every other charge. **Health insurance is decided in the fine print.**
:::

## The words that decide your claim

| Term | What it means | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| **Sum insured** | Max paid per policy year | Many planners suggest ₹10 lakh+ in a metro; more for a family |
| **Room-rent limit** | Cap on room charge per day | **No limit** or "single private room" |
| **Co-pay** | % of each claim you pay | **None**, ideally |
| **Sub-limits** | Caps on specific treatments (e.g. cataract ₹40,000) | Few or none |
| **Waiting periods** | Time before some things are covered | Short, and start early |
| **Restore / recharge** | Refills the sum insured after a claim in the same year | Useful for families |
| **No-claim bonus** | Cover grows for claim-free years | Nice extra |
| **Network hospitals** | Where cashless works | Good hospitals near you |

## Room-rent limits: the hidden trap

:::example
Policy: ₹5 lakh, room limit 1% = ₹5,000/day. Arjun takes an ₹8,000/day room for 3 days.

| Bill item | Billed | Paid by insurer |
|---|---|---|
| Room (3 days) | ₹24,000 | ₹15,000 (capped) |
| Doctor, nursing, OT (linked to room category) | ₹1,50,000 | ₹93,750 (cut to 5/8) |
| Medicines, consumables, tests | ₹66,000 | ₹66,000 |
| **Total** | **₹2,40,000** | **₹1,74,750** |

Arjun pays **₹65,250**, not the ₹9,000 room difference he expected. Hospitals charge more for everything in a costlier room category, so insurers cut those charges by the same ratio.
:::

## Waiting periods and the moratorium

- **Initial waiting**: usually ~30 days for illnesses. **Accidents are covered from day one.**
- **Specific diseases** (hernia, cataract, joint replacement…): typically 1–3 years.
- **Pre-existing diseases (PED)**: conditions you had before buying. IRDAI caps the wait at **3 years**.
- **Moratorium**: after **5 years of continuous cover**, the insurer can't reject a claim for non-disclosure or misrepresentation, except proven fraud.

**That's why you buy young.** Waiting periods run out while you're healthy, so cover is complete by the time you need it. Portability lets you switch insurers later without restarting them.

IRDAI's policyholder site explains health insurance rules and your rights. Check your own policy wording for its actual waiting periods.
:::

## Employer cover: good, not enough

- Ends when you leave, are laid off, or retire.
- Often small, with room-rent caps, and shared with family.
- Some insurers let you convert group cover to an individual policy when leaving. Ask HR, but don't rely on it.

**A sensible setup for Arjun:** keep the employer cover, add a **personal base policy** (say ₹5–10 lakh, no room-rent limit) and a **super top-up** (₹10–25 lakh above a ₹5–10 lakh deductible). Total cover becomes large at a modest premium, and stays with him across jobs.

## Claims: cashless vs reimbursement

- **Cashless**: at a **network hospital**, the hospital's insurance desk sends a pre-authorisation request. The insurer must decide **within 1 hour**, and process final discharge **within 3 hours** of the request.
- **Reimbursement**: you pay, then submit bills, discharge summary and reports within the deadline in your policy.
- Planned treatment: inform the insurer in advance. Emergency: inform within the time your policy specifies (often 24 hours).

:::trap
"Cheapest premium" policies often hide room-rent limits, co-pay and many sub-limits. Compare the **policy wording** (not the brochure). Read the exclusions and the "room rent" line before paying.
:::

:::dive Parents' health insurance: harder, and why to plan early
Insurers have no maximum entry age now, but premiums for people in their 50s and 60s are high, often come with co-pay, and pre-existing conditions like diabetes or BP mean waiting periods. Options: a senior-citizen plan with honest disclosure; adding them to your employer's cover if allowed; a separate "parents' medical fund" in a safe instrument. Discuss it with them now rather than in the hospital corridor. Module 17 covers family money.
:::

:::dive How a super top-up differs from a top-up
- **Top-up**: pays only when a **single** claim crosses the deductible.
- **Super top-up**: pays when the **total** of all claims in a year crosses the deductible.

Two ₹4 lakh hospital stays with a ₹5 lakh deductible: a top-up pays nothing (no single claim crossed ₹5 lakh); a super top-up pays ₹3 lakh. Prefer super top-ups.
:::
