## The big picture

SEBI groups schemes into categories, so funds within a category hold similar things. You'll mainly meet:

| Family | Holds | Typical use | How bad can it get? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Equity** | Shares | Goals 5–7+ years away | Falls of 30–50%+ have happened |
| **Debt** | Bonds, money-market paper | Near-term goals, stability | Small falls; occasionally sharp in a credit event |
| **Hybrid** | Mix of both | Smoother ride | In between |
| **Index funds / ETFs** | Copy an index (equity or debt) | Low-cost core holding | Same as the index |
| **Solution-oriented / others** | Retirement, children, FoFs, international | Specific needs | Varies |

## Equity funds

- **Large-cap**: the biggest 100 companies. Steadier, fewer surprises.
- **Mid-cap** (101st–250th) and **small-cap** (251st onwards): more growth potential, much bigger swings.
- **Flexi-cap / multi-cap**: mix of sizes, chosen by the manager (multi-cap must hold set minimums in each).
- **ELSS**: equity with a **3-year lock-in**; a tax deduction exists only under the old tax regime (Module 12).
- **Sectoral / thematic**: one sector or theme (banking, tech, PSU, defence). Concentrated; most people don't need them.

## Debt funds

- **Overnight / liquid / money market**: very short-term paper. For parking money for days to months. Low risk, **not zero**.
- **Short / medium / long duration, gilt**: longer bonds → bigger moves when rates change (Module 6, Lesson 5).
- **Credit risk funds**: lend to weaker borrowers for extra yield. That's where defaults hit.
- **Corporate bond / banking & PSU**: mostly high-rated borrowers.

:::dive The 2020 lesson: debt funds can freeze
In April 2020, Franklin Templeton India wound up six debt schemes holding lower-rated, hard-to-sell bonds when COVID panic dried up buyers. Investors' money was stuck for months and paid back in instalments over a few years. Lesson: "debt fund" doesn't mean "FD-like". For safety money, stick to overnight, liquid, or high-quality short-duration funds, or simply FDs.
:::

## Hybrid funds

They mix equity and debt within set ranges: **conservative** (mostly debt), **balanced advantage / dynamic** (shifts the mix with valuations), **aggressive** (mostly equity). **Arbitrage** funds use a low-risk trading strategy and behave more like debt, with equity-style taxation.

## Matching funds to goals

| When you need the money | Where it fits |
|---|---|
| Any time (emergency fund) | Savings account, sweep-in FD, liquid fund |
| Within 1–3 years | FD, liquid / short-duration debt |
| 3–5 years | Mostly debt, some hybrid or equity if you can accept a fall |
| 5–7+ years | Equity (e.g. a broad index fund) with some debt for stability |

:::example
Arjun's goals:

- Emergency fund ₹1.5 lakh → savings + sweep-in FD (Module 0)
- Bike in 18 months → FD or short-duration debt fund
- Retirement, 30+ years → broad equity index fund via SIP (Lesson 4)
- Sister's wedding contribution in 8 months → savings/FD only
:::

:::trap
Choosing funds from "top 5 funds this year" lists. Last year's winner is often last year's hottest category, which tends to mean-revert. Choose by **category fit for your goal**, then **low cost**, then consistency.
:::
