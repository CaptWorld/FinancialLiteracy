:::hook
In India, people are sold more insurance than almost anything else, yet many families face hospital bills or the loss of an earner with **too little cover**. The problem isn't how much insurance people buy. It's buying the **wrong kind**.
:::

## How insurance actually works

Say 1,000 people each pay ₹5,000 a year. Most never claim. A few need ₹10 lakh for surgery. The pool pays them. Each person traded a **small, certain cost** (the premium) for protection against a **big, uncertain one**.

That tells you the two rules:

1. **Insure big losses you can't absorb**: hospitalisation, your income if people depend on it, huge liabilities (e.g. a road accident).
2. **Don't insure small losses you can afford**: phone screens, mixer warranties, "return of premium" add-ons. You'd pay the insurer's costs and profit for little benefit.

## What a young salaried person usually needs

| Cover | Why | Who needs it |
|---|---|---|
| **Health insurance** (your own, plus employer's) | One hospital stay can cost lakhs | Everyone |
| **Term life insurance** | Replaces your income if you die | Anyone whose income others rely on (parents, spouse, kids, co-signed loans) |
| **Personal accident / disability** | Income if an accident stops you working | Especially if you commute by two-wheeler |
| **Motor insurance** | Third-party cover is legally required | Vehicle owners |
| **Home / contents** | Fire, theft, flood | If you own a home or valuable contents |

Not on the list: endowment, money-back, "guaranteed income" plans and most ULIPs. Lesson 4 explains why.

:::example
Arjun, 27: employer health cover ₹5 lakh, no personal policy, sends ₹8,000/month home, rides a bike to work, rents.

- **Health**: buy his own policy now, while healthy (Lesson 2).
- **Term**: yes, his parents depend on him (Lesson 3).
- **Personal accident**: worth considering, given the daily bike ride.
- **Home**: renting with few valuables, so low priority.
:::

## GST and insurance

Since **22 September 2025**, **individual** life and health insurance policies (including family floaters) are exempt from GST. **Group** policies, like your employer's cover, still carry 18% GST.

## The honesty rule

Fill the proposal form yourself and disclose everything: past illnesses, surgeries, medicines, smoking or alcohol, family history if asked. An agent who says "don't mention it" is setting your family up for a rejected claim. Non-disclosure is the most common reason claims fail.

:::dive Why the premium is more than the "expected" payout
If 1 in 1,000 people your age will make a ₹10 lakh claim, the "fair" premium is ₹1,000. You pay more because the insurer adds its costs, agent commission and profit. That's fine for big risks, where you're buying peace of mind and survival. For small risks it's a bad deal, because you'd lose ₹1 of every ₹3–4 paid to costs, every year. **Self-insure small risks with your emergency fund; buy insurance for catastrophic ones.**
:::
