:::hook
Finance sounds hard mostly because of vocabulary. **Twenty words cover most of what you'll read in news, bank apps and policy documents.** Learn these, and the rest of the course gets much easier.
:::

## What you have and what you owe

- **Asset**: something you own with money value. Savings, FDs, gold, a flat, mutual funds, even your EPF balance.
- **Liability**: money you owe. Loans, credit card dues, money borrowed from a friend.
- **Net worth = assets − liabilities.** It's your real financial position, whatever your salary is. Module 1 has you calculate yours.
- **Income**: money coming in (salary, interest, rent). **Expense**: money going out. **Savings** = income − expenses.

:::example
Arjun's take-home salary is ₹65,000 a month. His expenses (rent ₹16,000, food ₹9,000, bike EMI ₹3,877, phone and internet ₹1,000, travel and fun ₹10,000, sending home ₹8,000) total ₹47,877. **Savings: about ₹17,100 a month.**
:::

## Borrowing words

| Word | Meaning | Arjun's bike loan |
|---|---|---|
| **Principal** | Amount borrowed | ₹1,50,000 |
| **Interest rate** | Yearly cost of borrowing, "p.a." | 11% p.a. |
| **Tenure** | How long you take to repay | 4 years (48 months) |
| **EMI** | Equated Monthly Instalment: fixed monthly payment, part interest + part principal | ₹3,877 |
| **Outstanding** | Principal still left to repay | goes down every month |

- **Secured loan**: backed by **collateral**, something the lender can take if you don't pay (house, car, gold, FD). It's cheaper.
- **Unsecured loan**: no collateral (personal loan, credit card, education loan without security). It's costlier.
- **Default**: not paying as agreed. Lenders report it to credit bureaus, and it can block loans for years.
- **Co-applicant / guarantor**: someone who signs up to pay if you don't. Never sign as a guarantor casually; it's your liability too.

## Investing words

- **Return**: what an investment earns, usually shown as % per year.
- **Risk**: how uncertain the return is, including losing money. **Higher possible return almost always means higher risk.**
- **Volatility**: how much the value jumps up and down along the way.
- **Liquidity**: how fast you can get cash out at a fair value.
- **Diversification**: spreading money so one failure doesn't sink you. It's "don't put all your eggs in one basket".
- **Share / stock / equity**: a small piece of ownership in a company (Module 10).
- **Bond / debt**: a loan you give to a government or company for interest (Module 8).
- **Mutual fund**: many people's money pooled and invested by a professional fund house (Module 7).
- **Inflation**: prices rising over time (Module 1).

:::dive Why "higher return = higher risk" is almost a law
If something truly paid 15% a year with no risk, every bank, pension fund and billionaire would pour money into it until the price rose and the return fell. Safe, high returns don't survive in an open market. So when you see one advertised, ask: **where is the risk hiding?** It's usually in the company's ability to pay, in a lock-in, in fees, or in the advertiser's honesty. This is the single most useful question in personal finance.
:::

## Protection words

- **Insurance**: you pay a small, certain amount so an insurer pays a large, uncertain cost (death, hospitalisation, accident).
- **Premium**: what you pay, monthly or yearly.
- **Sum assured / cover / sum insured**: the maximum the insurer pays on a valid claim.
- **Policyholder / insured / nominee**: who owns the policy, whose life or health is covered, and who receives the money.

## Government and tax words

- **PAN**: your permanent tax ID. Needed for bank accounts, investments and salary. Never post it publicly.
- **TDS (Tax Deducted at Source)**: tax cut before money reaches you, e.g. from salary or FD interest.
- **ITR (Income Tax Return)**: the yearly form where you report income and settle tax (Module 12).

:::trap
Words used to sound safe: "assured", "guaranteed", "capital protected", "risk-free", "fixed returns". Read what is guaranteed, by whom, and over what period. A "guaranteed" plan from an insurer can still return less than inflation.
:::
