:::hook
Credit cards give banks a big chunk of their profit from people who "pay later". **The product is designed so the minimum due feels safe.** It isn't.
:::

## How a card bill works

- **Billing cycle**: about a month of purchases.
- **Statement date**: the bill is generated.
- **Due date**: usually a couple of weeks later.
- **Interest-free period**: from purchase to due date, if you pay the **total amount due**.

## The minimum due trap

If you pay anything less than the total:

- Interest is charged on the unpaid balance at the card's monthly rate (look for it in your card's MITC, the "Most Important Terms and Conditions").
- New purchases usually **lose the interest-free period** until you clear the balance.
- Late payments add fees and get reported to credit bureaus.

:::example
₹40,000 bill, you pay the ₹2,000 minimum. Say the card charges 3.5% a month (check yours). Next month's interest ≈ 3.5% × 38,000 ≈ ₹1,330, plus GST on it, plus interest on any new spends. That's over 40% a year.
:::

:::trap
**Cash withdrawals** on a credit card: a fee *and* interest from day one. **"No-cost EMI"**: read the final payable amount; processing fees and GST on interest often make it cost more than paying upfront. **Converting a big bill to EMI**: it's a loan; compare its rate with alternatives.
:::

## Using a card well

1. Set up **auto-debit for the total amount due**.
2. Spend only what you'd spend from your bank account anyway.
3. Keep utilisation moderate, well below your limit.
4. One or two cards are plenty. Rewards rarely justify more.

## Your credit score

A 3-digit number (commonly 300–900) built from your credit report. Lenders use it to approve loans and set your rate.

**What moves it:**
- Paying every EMI and card bill on time (biggest factor)
- Credit utilisation
- Length of credit history (don't close your oldest card without reason)
- Mix of credit types
- Recent applications (many in a short time looks risky)

Checking **your own** score doesn't hurt it.

:::checklive cibil
Get your free credit report (skip any paid plan). Then do the same on the other bureaus: Experian, Equifax, CRIF High Mark. Check every account listed is yours and every payment status is correct.
:::

:::checklive rbi-notifications
RBI has rules on how fast bureaus and lenders must fix disputed entries, and compensation if they're late. Search the notifications for "credit information" to read the current rules before you raise a dispute.
:::

:::task
1. Find your card's MITC and note the monthly interest rate and cash withdrawal fee.
2. Set auto-debit to **total amount due** if you haven't.
3. Download your free credit report from at least two bureaus. Any surprises?
4. Fill in the card and debt questions in **Me → My Money Snapshot**.
:::
