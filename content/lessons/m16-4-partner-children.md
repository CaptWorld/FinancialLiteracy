:::hook
Couples rarely fight about whether they have ₹5 lakh or ₹7 lakh. They fight about **surprises**: a loan nobody mentioned, money sent home without discussion, one person saving while the other spends. **Most money conflict is a conversation that didn't happen.**
:::

## Before (and soon after) marriage: the conversation

Cover, honestly:

- **Incomes and job stability**
- **Debts**: loans, card balances, EMIs
- **Family obligations**: support to parents, siblings' education or weddings
- **Big goals**: home, children, travel, career breaks
- **Money personalities**: saver vs spender, risk comfort, how decisions get made

Then agree on a **system**:

| System | How | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Fully joint | All income in shared accounts | High trust, similar habits |
| Fully separate | Split bills by agreement | Very different incomes or habits |
| **Yours, mine, ours** | Personal accounts + a joint account funded by agreed amounts | Most couples |

## The admin after marriage

1. **Nominees**: update everywhere (Lesson 3), and update your wills.
2. **Health insurance**: family floater or individual policies (Module 5). Check both employers' covers.
3. **Term insurance**: if either depends on the other's income, raise cover. Consider buying under **MWPA** so the money is protected for spouse/children (Module 5).
4. **Emergency fund**: resize for the household.
5. **Taxes**: each spouse files separately in India; plan investments in whose name (and clubbing rules for gifts between spouses) with care.

## Children: what changes

- **Term cover goes up**: dependants for 20+ years.
- **Health cover**: add the child; maternity cover often has long waiting periods, so plan early if needed.
- **Emergency fund**: larger.
- **Education** becomes a major goal.

:::example
Higher education costing **₹20 lakh** today, needed in **18 years**, at **8%** education inflation → ≈ **₹80 lakh**.

At an assumed 10% a year, that's a SIP of ≈ **₹13,200 a month** for 18 years, less with yearly step-ups. Safer pieces like PPF or Sukanya Samriddhi (for a daughter) can fill part of it, and the money should move to safe options in the last 3 years (glide path, Module 14).
:::

:::trap
"Child plans" and "child education policies" are usually endowment or ULIP products with a built-in premium waiver. The waiver is just term insurance inside an expensive wrapper. **Buy adequate term cover on the parent and invest separately for the child.** Calculate the IRR of any child plan you're offered (Module 5, Lesson 4).
:::

:::dive Supporting parents and children at once
Many Indians in their 30s and 40s support parents and children together, the "sandwich generation". The order still holds: your health and term insurance first (if you fall, everyone falls), parents' health cover or medical buffer second, emergency fund third, then children's goals and your own retirement **in parallel**. Children can borrow for education; you can't borrow for retirement.
:::
