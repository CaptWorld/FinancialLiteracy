:::hook
Two people make the same ₹2 lakh profit on the same fund. One pays about ₹40,000 in tax, the other under ₹10,000, and with a little planning it could be less. **The difference is timing and knowing the rules.**
:::

## Two kinds of investment income

1. **Slab-rate income**: added to your other income and taxed at your slab.
   - Savings account interest, FD/RD interest, NSC, SCSS, post office MIS
   - Dividends from shares and mutual funds
   - Gains on debt mutual funds bought on or after 1 April 2023
2. **Special-rate income**: taxed at fixed rates, separately.
   - Capital gains on listed equity and equity funds (and some other assets)

## Equity: shares and equity mutual funds

| Held | Type | Tax |
|---|---|---|
| 12 months or less | Short-term capital gain (STCG) | **20%** |
| More than 12 months | Long-term capital gain (LTCG) | **12.5%** on gains above **₹1.25 lakh** per year |

Plus 4% cess on the tax. No indexation.

:::example
Arjun sells units held 3 years with a **₹2 lakh** gain:
- Exempt: ₹1.25 lakh · Taxable: ₹75,000 · Tax: 12.5% = ₹9,375 + cess = **≈ ₹9,750**

Had he sold after 8 months: 20% × ₹2 lakh = ₹40,000 + cess = **≈ ₹41,600**.
:::

:::checklive incometax
Capital gains rates and exemptions are set by the Finance Act each year. Check the current ones before selling.
:::

## Debt funds, gold, and others

- **Debt mutual funds bought on or after 1 April 2023**: gains taxed at your **slab**, whatever the holding period. That's similar to an FD, with tax deferred until you sell.
- **Gold ETFs/funds, international funds, property, unlisted shares**: each has its own holding period and rate. Check before selling.
- **Crypto**: flat high rate, no loss set-off, TDS on transfers (Module 8).

## Tax-free (within their rules)

- **PPF**: interest and maturity
- **EPF**: interest on normal contribution levels (very high employee contributions make part of the interest taxable)
- **Sukanya Samriddhi**
- **SGB** gains on redemption at maturity
- Life insurance maturity: depends on conditions (premium levels, policy type), so check before assuming

## Smart, legal tax habits

- **Gain harvesting**: each financial year, sell and immediately rebuy enough equity fund units to book up to ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gains tax-free. Your purchase cost resets higher, cutting future tax. (Watch exit loads; the new units restart the 12-month clock.)
- **Loss harvesting**: book losses to offset gains. Short-term losses can offset short- or long-term gains; long-term losses only long-term gains.
- **Carry forward losses up to 8 years**, only if you file your return **on time**.
- **Hold equity beyond 12 months** where possible.
- **Spread big redemptions across financial years** to use the exemption more than once.

:::trap
Ignoring small income because "TDS was already cut". TDS on FD interest is often 10%, but if your slab is higher you owe more. Savings interest and dividends also need reporting. The department sees most of these in your AIS (Lesson 4).
:::

:::dive TDS on FD interest and the "no TDS" declaration
Banks deduct TDS on FD interest above a yearly threshold. If your total income is below the taxable limit, you can submit a self-declaration to the bank so it doesn't deduct TDS (historically Forms 15G for under-60s and 15H for seniors; form names may change under the new Act, so ask the bank). This matters most for parents with interest income but little tax. Never sign one if your income is actually taxable.
:::
