:::hook
Arjun's salary rose 40% in three years. His savings didn't. Nothing dramatic happened: a slightly nicer flat, more food delivery, a better phone, weekend trips. **Without a system, spending expands to fill whatever comes in.**
:::

## Emergency fund: the foundation

**Size:** 3–6 months of **essential** expenses (rent, food, EMIs, insurance, support to family, utilities).

Aim higher (6–12 months) if you're the **only earner**, **support family**, are in a **volatile job or industry**, or have **health issues** in the family.

**Where:** safe and fast. Savings account for a month's worth; the rest in a **sweep-in FD** or **liquid fund** (Modules 0, 7). Not in equity, PPF, or anything locked.

**When to use:** job loss, medical costs not covered by insurance, urgent family needs. Not sales or trips. Refill it after use.

## Pay yourself first: automate

Set up, dated 2–5 days after salary credit:

1. **SIPs** for long-term goals (Module 7)
2. **RD / auto-sweep** for near goals
3. **Emergency fund top-up** until it's full
4. **Support for family**, if regular

What's left in the account is genuinely yours to spend, with no guilt and no tracking every rupee.

:::example
Arjun's salary arrives on the 1st (₹65,000). On the 3rd:

| Auto-transfer | ₹ |
|---|---|
| Index fund SIP (retirement) | 5,000 |
| RD (bike, 2 years) | 6,400 |
| Emergency fund top-up (until ₹2.9 lakh) | 5,000 |
| Home | 8,000 |
| **Left for rent and spending** | **40,600** |

Rent ₹16,000 + fixed bills leave about ₹20,000 for food, travel and fun. If that's too tight, he adjusts the goals, not the automation.
:::

## A starting budget split

**50 / 30 / 20**: about half to needs, 30% to wants, 20%+ to savings. It's a starting point, not a rule. In expensive cities, needs may be 60%; when supporting family, "needs" includes them. **Tools → Budget** helps you see your actual split.

## Beating lifestyle creep

- **Split every raise**: at least half to savings (step up SIPs that month), the rest to enjoy.
- **Wait 30 days** before big non-essential purchases.
- **Upgrade one thing at a time**, deliberately.

:::trap
"I'll start saving once I earn more." Savings rates rarely rise on their own as income rises; habits do the work. Starting with even 10% now beats planning 30% later.
:::

:::dive Why automation works better than willpower
Every manual decision to save competes with something you want today, and today usually wins. Automation makes saving the default and spending the deliberate choice. Studies of retirement plans that enrol people automatically (rather than asking them to opt in) consistently show far higher participation. The same psychology works for one person and their salary account.
:::
