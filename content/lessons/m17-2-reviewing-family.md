:::hook
A common picture in Indian families: a bank relationship manager who "handles everything", a stack of LIC and private policies bought for tax, several FDs spread oddly, a rented-out flat, and a home loan nobody has re-checked in years. **None of it is a disaster. All of it can usually be improved.**
:::

## Arjun's father: the setup (illustrative)

- **Mutual funds**: 7 equity funds, regular plans, via his bank
- **Insurance**: two endowment policies (₹60,000/year), a ₹3 lakh family floater
- **FDs**: ₹9 lakh in one small bank, ₹3 lakh in another
- **Rent**: ₹20,000/month from a small flat
- **Home loan**: ₹18 lakh outstanding at 9.6%, 11 years left

## Mutual funds: start with the CAS

Get his **CAS** (Module 7) and look at:

- **Regular vs direct**: regular plans pay yearly commission.
- **Overlap**: seven large/flexi-cap funds often hold the same top companies.
- **Purpose**: does each fund have a goal, and does the equity share suit his age and needs (Module 14)?
- **Nominees** on every folio.

**Possible plan:** new SIPs into 1–2 direct-plan index or broad funds; existing units switched gradually, using the ₹1.25 lakh yearly LTCG exemption and avoiding exit loads (Module 12).

## Policies: the IRR test

For each endowment/money-back plan (Module 5, Lesson 4): premiums paid and remaining, maturity value, surrender value, **IRR**. Then compare **continue**, **paid-up** and **surrender**. Before stopping anything, make sure **health cover is adequate**. A ₹3 lakh floater is thin for parents in a city; consider a top-up or super top-up if he's still insurable.

## FDs: spread and check TDS

₹9 lakh in one small bank is above the insurance limit (Module 15, Lesson 2). Spread across strong banks, consider **SCSS** for regular income once eligible (Module 8), and check whether TDS is being deducted unnecessarily on interest (Module 12).

## Rent: taxed, with a standard deduction

:::example
Rent ₹2,40,000/year; municipal tax paid ₹10,000.
- Net annual value = 2,40,000 − 10,000 = **₹2,30,000**
- Standard deduction (30%) = ₹69,000
- **Taxable from the property ≈ ₹1,61,000**, added to his income at his slab (home-loan interest on a let-out property can reduce it further, within limits)

Keep the rent agreement, receipts and the tenant's details. Rent above certain levels can also show up in the tax department's records, so it must be reported.
:::

## Home loan: reprice it

At **9.6%** with ₹18 lakh and 11 years left, a cut to around 8.4% (if his profile qualifies) saves a meaningful amount every month. Ask the bank to **reduce his spread** first; compare a **balance transfer** if they won't (Module 4, Lesson 2). Check whether prepaying from idle FDs makes sense against the FD's after-tax return.

:::trap
Don't move everything at once. Surrender charges, exit loads, capital-gains tax and a parent's sense of control all argue for **gradual, agreed changes**. A fee-only adviser or CA can make the plan, and saves family arguments.
:::

:::task
1. With your parent's consent, download their CAS and list each fund as regular/direct and its category.
2. Pick one insurance policy and calculate its IRR together.
3. Check the home-loan rate on the latest statement and compare it with the lender's current rate for new borrowers.
:::
