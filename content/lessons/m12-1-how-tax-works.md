:::hook
Many new employees assume "30% of my salary goes to tax". For a large share of salaried Indians the truth today is very different: **after the standard deduction and rebate, income up to about ₹12.75 lakh of salary pays no income tax at all in the new regime.**
:::

## The new Act, in one paragraph

From **1 April 2026**, the **Income-tax Act, 2025** replaced the 1961 Act. The rules are largely similar but renumbered and simplified. Key name changes you'll see: **tax year** (instead of previous year/assessment year), **Form 130** (instead of Form 16), **Form 168** (instead of Form 26AS). Module 2, Lesson 3 covers these documents.

## How slabs work: marginal rates

Each rate applies **only to the slice of income inside that band**.

**New regime, tax year 2026-27** (Budget 2026 kept these unchanged):

| Taxable income | Rate on that slice |
|---|---|
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | Nil |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

Then:

- **Rebate**: if taxable income (excluding special-rate income like capital gains) is **up to ₹12 lakh**, tax becomes **nil**. Just above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief stops tax from jumping unfairly.
- **Cess**: add **4%** of the tax.
- **Surcharge**: only on very high incomes.

## Worked examples

:::example
**Arjun**, gross salary ₹9.6 lakh, new regime:

- Standard deduction: − ₹75,000 → taxable **₹8.85 lakh**
- Tax before rebate: 5% of 4L (₹20,000) + 10% of 85,000 (₹8,500) = ₹28,500
- Taxable income ≤ ₹12 lakh → **rebate → tax nil**

If his employer still deducts TDS, he gets it back as a refund when he files. Usually employers calculate correctly from his declaration.
:::

:::example
**Priya**, gross salary ₹18 lakh, new regime:

- Taxable: 18L − 75,000 = **₹17.25 lakh**
- 5% × 4L = ₹20,000 · 10% × 4L = ₹40,000 · 15% × 4L = ₹60,000 · 20% × 1.25L = ₹25,000
- Tax = ₹1,45,000 + 4% cess = **₹1,50,800** (about 8.4% of gross salary)
:::

## TDS and the final settlement

Throughout the year, tax is collected in advance as **TDS**: from salary (by your employer), from FD interest (by banks, above a threshold), and some other payments. At year-end your **return (ITR)** works out the actual tax on all income. Paid too much → **refund**. Too little → pay the balance before filing.

:::trap
Income from **other sources** is taxable too: savings interest, FD interest, dividends, capital gains, freelance income. TDS on salary doesn't cover it. The department already sees most of it in your AIS (Lesson 4), so leaving it out of your return invites a notice.
:::

:::dive Why the rebate doesn't cover capital gains
The ₹12 lakh rebate applies to normal income taxed at slab rates. Income taxed at **special rates**, like long-term gains on equity (Lesson 3), is taxed separately and isn't wiped out by the rebate in the same way. So someone with ₹10 lakh salary and large equity gains may still owe tax on the gains. Check the department's calculator when you have capital gains.
:::
