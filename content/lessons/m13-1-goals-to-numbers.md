:::hook
"I want to buy a house someday" isn't a goal. **"₹25 lakh down payment in 6 years"** is. Once a goal has a number and a date, the rest is arithmetic, and the arithmetic often reveals that something has to change: the date, the amount, or how much you save.
:::

## Step 1: write goals with numbers and dates

:::example
Arjun's list (amounts in today's rupees):

| Goal | Today's cost | When | Inflation assumed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency fund (6 months of expenses) | ₹2.9 lakh | Now, top priority | — |
| Bike upgrade | ₹1.5 lakh | 2 years | 5% |
| Parents' medical buffer | ₹5 lakh | 3 years | 8–10% (healthcare) |
| Home down payment | ₹25 lakh | 6 years | 6% (property) |
| Retirement | (Module 16) | 30+ years | 6% |
| Self-funding extras (subscription + giving) | ₹2,000/month | 10 years | 6% (Lesson 4) |
:::

## Step 2: future cost

**Future cost = today's cost × (1 + inflation)^years**

- Down payment: 25 lakh × 1.06⁶ ≈ **₹35.5 lakh**
- Bike: 1.5 lakh × 1.05² ≈ **₹1.65 lakh**

Use **higher inflation** for education and healthcare, which usually rise faster than CPI (Module 6).

## Step 3: where the money sits (by time)

| Years to goal | Sensible home for the money |
|---|---|
| < 1–3 | Savings, FD, RD, liquid or short-duration debt funds |
| 3–5 | Mostly debt; a small equity share if you can accept a dip |
| 5–7+ | Equity index funds with some debt; shift to safe options in the last 2–3 years |

## Step 4: monthly amount

Use **Tools → Goal planner** with a **conservative** return: ~6–7% for safe options, ~10% or less for equity over long periods. Then check whether it's realistic.

:::example
- **Bike**: ₹1.65 lakh in 2 years at ~6.5% → **≈ ₹6,400/month** (RD/short debt)
- **Parents' medical buffer**: ₹5 lakh in 3 years at ~6.5% → **≈ ₹12,500/month**
- **Down payment**: ₹35.5 lakh in 6 years at ~8% → **≈ ₹38,300/month**

Total ≈ ₹57,000 a month against ₹17,000 of savings. **Arjun can't do all of this.** He must prioritise, push the house further out, consider a smaller down payment, and grow income. That conversation with himself is the point of the exercise.
:::

## Step 5: prioritise

1. **Emergency fund** and **insurance** (Modules 0, 5): non-negotiable.
2. **Near, essential goals** (family health, committed obligations).
3. **Retirement**: start small but start now; time is its biggest ingredient.
4. **Wants** (bike, travel, house timing): adjust size and date to fit.

:::trap
Planning with 15% expected returns "because the fund gave that last year". High assumptions produce tiny monthly SIPs that never reach the goal. Assume less, review yearly, and enjoy any surplus.
:::

:::task
1. Enter your goals in **Me → Goals** with today's cost, date and inflation.
2. Use **Tools → Goal planner** for each and note the monthly amount.
3. Add them up. If the total exceeds what you save, rank goals and change dates or amounts until the plan fits.
:::
