:::hook
"I want my Claude subscription and my monthly donation to pay for themselves." It's a lovely goal, and a great way to understand how all passive income actually works: **a big enough pot, a cautious withdrawal rate, and years of patience.**
:::

## The basic formula

**Pot needed ≈ yearly cost ÷ withdrawal rate**

The **withdrawal rate** is how much of the pot you take out each year. Lower is safer.

:::example
Arjun's recurring extras: a subscription plus regular giving ≈ **₹2,000 a month = ₹24,000 a year** (illustrative; put your real numbers in the tool).

| Withdrawal rate | Pot needed today |
|---|---|
| 5% | ₹4.8 lakh |
| **4%** | **₹6 lakh** |
| 3.5% | ₹6.9 lakh |
| 3% | ₹8 lakh |
:::

## Add inflation

Prices rise, so subscriptions and the cost of meaningful giving do too. At 6% a year, ₹24,000 becomes **≈ ₹43,000 in 10 years**, and at 4% the pot needed becomes **≈ ₹10.7 lakh**.

## How long to build it

:::example
A dedicated SIP at an assumed **10% a year** (not guaranteed):

| Target | Years | Monthly SIP |
|---|---|---|
| ₹6 lakh (today's cost) | 10 | ≈ ₹2,900 |
| ₹10.7 lakh (inflation-adjusted) | 10 | ≈ ₹5,200 |

Starting earlier, or stepping up yearly (Module 7), makes it much easier.
:::

Track it in **Tools → Self-funding**: list your extras, pick a withdrawal rate, and update the invested amount. Today's page shows your progress.

## What can go wrong (be honest)

- **Bad years early (sequence risk)**: if the pot falls 30% just as you start withdrawing, it may never recover enough.
- **Costs rise faster than assumed**: subscriptions can jump sharply in price.
- **Taxes** on gains or interest reduce what you can withdraw (Module 12).
- **Withdrawal rates aren't laws**: figures like "4%" come from historical studies, mostly of US markets; Indian inflation and returns differ. Treat them as rough guides.

**Sensible guardrails:** use 3–4%; begin withdrawals only when the pot is comfortably above the target; keep a year's worth of withdrawals in a safe fund so you never sell equity in a crash to pay a bill.

:::trap
"Passive income" courses and trading groups promise this in months. Real passive income is the **result** of years of active saving and investing. Trading isn't passive and, for most people, isn't income (Module 11). Dividend-chasing stocks aren't a shortcut either.
:::

:::dive Keep giving now
Don't pause generosity until the pot is ready. Keep giving from salary at whatever level you can, and let the pot gradually take over. Some people start by making just the subscription self-funding (a smaller target), celebrate that, then extend it to giving. Small wins keep long goals alive.
:::
