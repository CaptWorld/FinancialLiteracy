"Bonus 1:1 announced, stock to double!" trends every few months. Arjun's colleague bought on the news. A week later he had twice the shares at half the price, and the same money. **Learning what actually changes value saves you from most market noise.**

## Placing orders

- **Market order**: buy/sell now at the best available price. Fine for very liquid large-caps, risky for thinly traded shares.
- **Limit order**: you set the price; it executes only at that price or better.
- **Delivery vs intraday**: delivery means you actually take the shares into your demat. Intraday positions must be closed the same day (Module 11 explains why that's mostly a losing game).

## Corporate actions, decoded

| Action | What happens | Value created? |
|---|---|---|
| **Dividend** | Cash paid per share; price drops ~by the dividend on the record date | No new value; profits handed to you (taxable) |
| **Bonus issue** | Free extra shares in a ratio (e.g. 1:1) | No, same value, more shares |
| **Stock split** | Each share split into several (e.g. 1:5) | No, same value, lower price per share |
| **Buyback** | Company buys back its own shares | Helps remaining holders only if the price paid is sensible |
| **Rights issue** | Existing holders offered new shares, usually at a discount | Company raises money; you must invest more or get diluted |

## Tips, finfluencers and pump-and-dumps

The pattern is consistent:

1. Operators quietly buy a small, thinly traded share.
2. Channels and "advisers" hype it: urgent targets, screenshots of profits.
3. Followers rush in; the price rises.
4. Operators sell into that demand; the price collapses.

SEBI has barred unregistered "advisers" and finfluencers and acted against tip channels. **Paid stock advice requires SEBI registration** (Research Analyst or Investment Adviser). Check before trusting anyone.

:::checklive scores
If a SEBI-registered intermediary wrongs you, complain through SCORES. For scams by unregistered people, report to SEBI and on cybercrime.gov.in.
:::

## Learn on paper first

Before risking real money in individual stocks, run a **paper portfolio** for 6–12 months in a spreadsheet or **Me → Notes**:

| Date | Stock | Price | Amount (imaginary) | Why (3 lines) | What would prove me wrong | Review notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Every month, compare your paper portfolio with a Nifty 50 index fund. Most beginners find they trail the index. That's an inexpensive and valuable lesson.

:::trap
"Learning by losing" with real money is expensive. Start with an index fund SIP. Graduate to a small, capped stock portion only after months of honest paper trading and Modules 9–10.
:::

:::dive Dividends vs growth: what's better?
Neither, in itself. A company paying dividends hands cash to you (taxed at your slab); one reinvesting profits at high returns (high ROCE, Module 9) can compound value inside the business. What matters is whether retained profits are used well. A high dividend yield from a shrinking business is not a bargain.
:::
