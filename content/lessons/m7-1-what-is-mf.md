:::hook
Arjun's colleague says, "Mutual funds are risky, the company can run away with your money." His uncle says, "Mutual funds are like FDs, but better." **Both are wrong**, and the truth is the foundation for everything else in this module.
:::

## Pooling, in one picture

Ten lakh people each put in a few thousand rupees. The pool is now thousands of crores. A professional team at an **AMC (Asset Management Company)** invests it in a spread of shares, bonds or both, according to the fund's stated rules. Each investor owns **units**: a slice of the pool.

What you get:

- **Diversification**: one small SIP owns a bit of 50–100+ companies or bonds.
- **Professional management** (or an automatic index-following one, Lesson 3).
- **Liquidity**: most funds let you sell any business day.
- **Regulation**: SEBI sets rules on what each fund type can hold, costs, disclosure and conduct.

## NAV and units

**NAV (Net Asset Value) per unit = (value of everything the fund holds − its liabilities) ÷ units outstanding**

It's calculated after markets close every business day.

:::example
Arjun invests ₹10,000 at NAV ₹50 → **200 units**.

- NAV rises to ₹55 → 200 × 55 = **₹11,000** (+10%)
- NAV falls to ₹45 → 200 × 45 = **₹9,000** (−10%)

His **units** don't change unless he buys or sells. Only the NAV moves.
:::

## Who holds your money: the structure

| Role | Who | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Sponsor** | Group that sets up the fund | Brings capital and credibility |
| **Trustees** | Independent board | Protect unitholders' interests |
| **AMC** | Fund house | Manages the money, for a fee |
| **Custodian** | Usually a bank | Holds the actual shares and bonds |
| **RTA** | CAMS / KFintech | Keeps the unit records and statements |
| **SEBI** | Regulator | Rules for all of the above |

So the fund's assets are **not the AMC's money**. If an AMC fails, the assets remain with the custodian for unitholders. **But nothing protects you from markets.** If the shares or bonds fall, the NAV falls. Mutual funds have no deposit insurance and no guaranteed returns.

## The riskometer

Every scheme carries SEBI's **riskometer**: Low → Low to Moderate → Moderate → Moderately High → High → Very High. Most equity funds are "Very High". That doesn't mean "bad". It means **the value can swing a lot, including big falls**, so the money should be for long-term goals.

:::trap
"NFO at ₹10, get in early!" A new fund's unit price says nothing about value or future returns. NFOs are often launched when a theme is popular, after it has already risen. An existing fund with years of track record and lower costs is usually the better choice.
:::

:::dive Why "past returns" in ads come with a warning
Every mutual fund ad says "Mutual fund investments are subject to market risks…" because past returns don't predict future ones, especially for a fund that topped a 1-year ranking. Studies of fund rankings (including SPIVA India, Lesson 3) show few top-quartile funds stay on top. Choose funds on costs, category fit and consistency, not on last year's winner.
:::
