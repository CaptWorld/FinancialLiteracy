## What an exchange rate is

The **exchange rate** is the price of one currency in another, like **₹ per US dollar**.

- **Rupee weakens / depreciates**: more ₹ per $ (e.g. 85 → 88).
- **Rupee strengthens / appreciates**: fewer ₹ per $.

Like any price, it moves with demand and supply. If people and companies want more dollars (to pay for oil, gadgets, foreign fees, or because foreign investors are leaving), the rupee weakens.

## Why the rupee has tended to weaken

Over the long run the main driver is **inflation difference**. If prices in India rise about 4–5% a year and in the US about 2%, the rupee tends to lose a few percent a year against the dollar. It isn't a steady line, though: there are years of sharp falls and years of stability. Other drivers:

- **Trade**: India imports more goods than it exports, especially **crude oil**, electronics and gold.
- **Capital flows**: foreign investors buying Indian shares and bonds bring dollars in; when they sell, dollars leave.
- **Remittances**: Indians abroad send home large amounts, a big support for the rupee.
- **Global mood and US rates**: when the US Federal Reserve raises rates or the world is scared, money flows to the dollar.

## RBI's role

RBI doesn't fix the rupee at a level. It **smooths sharp swings** by buying or selling dollars from its **foreign exchange reserves**, and keeps reserves as insurance, enough to pay for months of imports in a crisis.

RBI publishes the reference rate and weekly forex reserves on its site.
:::

## How it reaches you

| You… | Weaker rupee means |
|---|---|
| Fill petrol, pay cab fares | Pressure for costlier fuel over time (oil is priced in dollars), though taxes and pricing policy smooth it |
| Plan foreign education or travel | Costlier in rupees, so plan for it |
| Buy an iPhone or a laptop | Imported parts get costlier |
| Work in IT services | Employers earn more rupees per dollar; helps hiring and pay |
| Receive money from relatives abroad | More rupees per dollar |
| Invest in US stocks or international funds | Your returns in rupees get a boost from the currency |

:::example
Arjun's cousin's $30,000 course, at ₹88/$ today = **₹26.4 lakh**. If the rupee weakens 3% a year for 5 years (illustrative), the same $30,000 costs about **₹30.6 lakh**. For any foreign-currency goal, plan with **both** the fee inflation and an assumed rupee decline.
:::

:::dive Current account deficit, in one paragraph
The **current account** tracks money flowing in and out for goods, services, income and remittances. India usually runs a small **deficit**: it pays out more than it earns, mostly due to oil and gold imports, offset partly by IT exports and remittances. The gap is financed by foreign investment. When the deficit widens sharply (say oil spikes) and foreign money leaves at the same time, the rupee comes under pressure. That's the mix behind most rupee "crises" in the news.
:::

:::dive Why gold behaves differently in India
Gold is priced globally in dollars. In rupees, gold's price = dollar gold price × ₹/$ rate. So when the rupee weakens, gold in India rises even if global gold doesn't. That's one reason Indian families have long seen gold as a hedge, and one reason gold imports weigh on the rupee. Module 8 covers gold as an investment.
:::
