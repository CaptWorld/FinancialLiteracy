:::hook
Most salaried people say "I haven't started investing". Then they open their EPF passbook and find lakhs already there, growing at a government-declared rate. **You've probably been investing in a safe asset since your first salary. The question is what to add.**
:::

## PPF in practice

**PPF (Public Provident Fund)**: open at a post office or most banks (online too). One account per person.

| Feature | Rule |
|---|---|
| Deposit | ₹500 to ₹1.5 lakh per financial year, in one go or many |
| Rate | Set by government each quarter (7.1% for Oct–Dec 2026) |
| Interest | Calculated monthly on the lowest balance between the **5th and month-end**, credited yearly |
| Term | 15 years, then extend in blocks of 5 years (with or without new deposits) |
| Access | Loans in years 3–6; partial withdrawals from year 7 |
| Tax | Interest and maturity **tax-free**; deduction on deposits only in the **old regime** |
| Safety | Backed by the Government of India |

:::example
₹1.5 lakh deposited each year by **5 April**, for 15 years at 7.1%:

- Invested: ₹22.5 lakh
- Maturity: ≈ **₹40.7 lakh**, all tax-free

For comparison, the same deposits in a 7% FD taxed at 30% (4.9% after tax) grow to about ₹33.7 lakh. Rates can change; this assumes they stay put.
:::

## EPF: the safe investment you already have

From Module 2: **12% of your basic pay** goes to EPF, plus your employer's share (part of it goes to the pension scheme, EPS). EPFO declares the interest rate each year. It has historically been higher than most FDs and is **tax-free** for normal contribution levels.

- **Don't withdraw EPF when you change jobs**: transfer it (same UAN), or the compounding resets.
- **Check the passbook** a few times a year to confirm employer deposits are actually reaching it.

:::checklive epfo
EPFO announces the interest rate for each financial year. Check the latest declared rate.
:::

## VPF: more EPF, voluntarily

**VPF (Voluntary Provident Fund)**: ask HR to deduct more than 12% into EPF. Same rate, same safety, same lock-in rules as EPF. Interest on employee contributions above a yearly limit becomes taxable, which matters only for high contributions.

**When it makes sense:** you want more safe, long-term money and you're unlikely to need it before retirement. Compared with PPF: VPF is automatic from salary; PPF is separate, stays with you across jobs, and has its own ₹1.5 lakh limit.

## Where these fit

| Goal horizon | Role of PPF/EPF/VPF |
|---|---|
| Emergency fund | **No**, because they're locked |
| 3–7 year goals | PPF partly (withdrawals from year 7); usually FDs/debt funds fit better |
| Retirement, 15+ years | **Yes**, as the safe/debt part, alongside equity for growth |

:::trap
Treating PPF/EPF as the whole retirement plan. They're safe and tax-free, but at ~7–8% they mostly protect against inflation rather than build large real wealth over 30 years. Most long-term plans pair them with an equity portion (Module 7).
:::

:::dive How much is "safe" vs "growth"?
A common starting point is to hold a mix of equity and safe assets based on your goal horizon and how much fall you can stomach. For example, someone young with a 30-year horizon might hold a large equity share, with EPF/PPF as the safe part. There's no single right number; Module 14 covers asset allocation properly. The key idea now: EPF already counts as part of your safe allocation.
:::
