:::hook
After a great year for shares, everyone feels like adding more equity. After a crash, everyone wants to get out. **Rebalancing makes you do the opposite, calmly, by rule.** That's why it works, and why it's hard.
:::

## Why your mix drifts

:::example
Arjun's target: **70% equity / 30% debt**, ₹10 lakh.

**A strong year**: equity +30%, debt +7%

| | Start | End |
|---|---|---|
| Equity | ₹7,00,000 | ₹9,10,000 |
| Debt | ₹3,00,000 | ₹3,21,000 |
| Equity share | 70% | **73.9%** |

To restore 70/30: move about **₹48,300** from equity to debt, or simply direct the next few months' SIPs to debt.

**A crash year**: equity −30%, debt +7% → equity ₹4.9L, debt ₹3.21L, equity share **60.4%**. To restore 70/30: move about **₹77,700** into equity.
:::

## When to rebalance

Pick one rule and write it down:

- **Calendar**: once a year, on a fixed date (e.g. every April with your SIP step-up).
- **Bands**: whenever equity drifts more than **±5 percentage points** from target.
- Or both: check yearly, act only if outside the band.

Frequent rebalancing adds costs and taxes without much benefit.

## How to rebalance cheaply

1. **New money first**: send SIPs, bonuses or debt maturities to the underweight side.
2. **Switch within tax limits**: use the yearly ₹1.25 lakh equity LTCG exemption; avoid selling units held under 12 months (Module 12).
3. **Check exit loads** before switching funds.
4. **Inside EPF/PPF** you can't rebalance, so adjust around them with your other holdings.

:::trap
"I'll rebalance when the market looks better." That's market timing with extra steps. The point of a rule is to act **without** a forecast, especially in a crash when buying equity feels worst.
:::

:::dive Rebalancing isn't about maximising returns
In a long bull market, never rebalancing would have left you with more equity and more money, and more risk than you signed up for. Rebalancing's job is to **keep your risk where you chose it**, so a crash hurts by the amount you planned for, not more. The "buy low, sell high" effect is a bonus.
:::
