Arjun needs ₹3 lakh for his sister's wedding. Lender A says **12%**, Lender B says **12.75%**. A looks cheaper. Read the Key Facts Statement and A turns out to cost about **15.1% a year**, while B costs about **13%**.

## The Key Facts Statement (KFS)

Since October 2024, **every retail and MSME loan** from banks and NBFCs must come with a **KFS** in a standard format before you sign. It shows:

- Loan amount, interest rate, type (fixed or floating), tenure, EMI
- **All fees and charges**: processing fee, insurance, documentation, third-party charges
- **APR (Annual Percentage Rate)**: the all-in yearly cost including fees
- **Total amount you'll pay** and the repayment schedule
- Penal charges, prepayment terms, cooling-off period, grievance contacts

**Any charge not in the KFS can't be levied without your explicit consent.** Ask for the KFS before signing, and keep a copy.

## Comparing on APR

:::example
| | Offer A | Offer B |
|---|---|---|
| Headline rate | 12% | 12.75% |
| Processing fee | 2% + 18% GST = ₹7,080 | ₹999 + GST = ₹1,179 |
| "Loan protection insurance" | ₹6,000 (deducted) | none |
| Arjun actually receives | ₹2,86,920 | ₹2,98,821 |
| EMI (3 years) | ₹9,964 | ₹10,072 |
| **APR** | **≈ 15.1%** | **≈ 13.0%** |

A's EMI is lower, but Arjun repays it on ₹3 lakh while receiving only ₹2.87 lakh. That's why its APR is higher. Try it in **Tools → Compare loans**.
:::

## Hidden extras to question

- **Bundled insurance**: often "mandatory" on the call and optional in reality. Ask in writing. If you do need cover, a term plan you buy yourself (Module 5) is usually far cheaper.
- **GST**: added to fees and penal charges, not to the interest.
- **Penal charges**: since 2024, lenders can charge a reasonable, disclosed **penal charge** for late payment, but can't add penal interest to your rate or charge interest on the penalty.
- **Prepayment and foreclosure charges**: none on floating-rate loans taken by individuals for non-business purposes. Fixed-rate loans may have them. Lesson 5 covers this.

## "No-cost EMI"

The interest still exists. Usually either the retailer removes the discount you'd get paying upfront, or the bank adds a processing fee plus GST on the interest it waives. **Compare: total of all EMIs + fees vs the upfront price.** If they match, fine. Remember it still counts as a loan on your credit report.

:::dive How APR is worked out
APR is the interest rate at which the money you **actually receive** today equals the present value of all the EMIs you'll pay. Lender A gives ₹2,86,920 and takes 36 × ₹9,964. The rate that balances those two is about 15.1% a year. It's the same idea as the EMI formula, solved for the rate instead of the EMI. This is also how SEBI and RBI want costs compared everywhere: on what you actually get versus what you actually pay.
:::

:::trap
Instant loan apps and "pay later" offers often show a daily or monthly cost ("₹30 a day") and skip the APR. If a lender won't give you a KFS with an APR, don't borrow from them.
:::
