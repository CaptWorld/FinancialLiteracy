:::hook
In June a PDF arrives from HR called "Form 16". Most people forward it to a CA or a filing app without opening it. **That PDF is your proof that tax was paid, and mistakes in it become your problem, not your employer's.**
:::

## Big change: a new Income-tax Act

The **Income-tax Act, 2025** came into force on **1 April 2026**, replacing the 1961 Act. For you this mostly means **new names and numbers**:

| What | Old name (FY 2025-26 and earlier) | New name (tax year 2026-27 onward) |
|---|---|---|
| Salary TDS certificate | Form 16 | **Form 130** |
| Annual tax statement | Form 26AS | **Form 168** |
| Investment declaration to employer | Form 12BB | Form 124 |
| The year | Previous year / Assessment year | **Tax year** |

Section numbers changed too (e.g. what people call "80C" has a new section number). Most articles online still use the old numbers. Both are fine to recognise; just know which year you're talking about.

:::checklive it-act-2025
Skim the official note so you can recognise the new terms. Don't memorise section numbers; look them up when needed.
:::

## How TDS works

1. At the start of the year you tell your employer your **regime** (new or old) and, for old, your planned investments/rent.
2. Your employer estimates your annual tax and deducts it in monthly instalments.
3. Near year-end you submit proofs. TDS is adjusted in the last months.
4. After the year, you get **Form 16 / Form 130** summarising salary and TDS.

## The two regimes

- **New regime** (default): lower slab rates, very few deductions.
- **Old regime**: higher rates, but deductions such as HRA, the popular investment deduction (PPF, ELSS, EPF, life insurance), health insurance premium, home-loan interest.

Which is better depends on your numbers. Many salaried people with little rent and few deductions pay less in the new regime; people paying high rent with large deductions sometimes pay less in the old one.

:::checklive it-salaried
Look at the current slab tables for both regimes for your age group. Then run your own numbers in the official calculator.
:::

## HRA in one paragraph

If you're in the **old regime** and **pay rent**, part of your HRA is tax-free. The exempt amount is the **lowest** of: (1) HRA actually received, (2) rent paid minus 10% of basic, (3) a percentage of basic that depends on your city. Keep rent receipts and a rent agreement; if annual rent is above a threshold you'll need the landlord's PAN.

## The documents and the cross-check

- **Form 16 / 130**: what your employer says they paid you and deducted.
- **Form 26AS / 168**: what the tax department actually received against your PAN.
- **AIS**: everything the department knows about you: salary, savings interest, FD interest, dividends, mutual fund/share sales, large purchases.

:::checklive it-26as-168
Follow the steps to open your annual tax statement for last year. Does total TDS match your Form 16?
:::

:::checklive it-ais-faq
Read what AIS contains, then open yours on the e-filing portal. Look for interest and dividends you might have forgotten.
:::

:::trap
Filing from Form 16 alone. Savings interest, FD interest and dividends are taxable and appear in AIS. Leaving them out is the most common reason salaried people get notices.
:::

:::task
1. Log in to the income tax portal.
2. Download last year's Form 26AS and AIS.
3. Compare total TDS: Form 16 vs 26AS. Match? (Yes/No)
4. Find any savings/FD interest or dividends in AIS. Did you report them last year?
5. Note which regime you chose this year and why, in **Me → Notes**.
:::
