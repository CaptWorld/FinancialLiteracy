:::hook
"My RSUs are worth more than my salary" sounds like winning. Then the company has a bad year: layoffs and a falling share price, at once. **Employee equity is real money, taxed twice, and riskier than it feels because it's tied to your job.**
:::

## The two common types

| | ESOP (Employee Stock Option) | RSU (Restricted Stock Unit) |
|---|---|---|
| What you get | Right to **buy** shares at a fixed **exercise price** | **Shares** themselves |
| When | After **vesting** (often over 3–4 years) | As they vest |
| You pay | Exercise price (+ tax) | Nothing to receive (tax still due) |
| Common at | Startups, Indian listed firms | Large listed companies, MNCs |

## How they're taxed in India

**1. At exercise (ESOP) or vesting (RSU): salary tax**
The market value minus anything you paid counts as a **perquisite**, taxed at your slab with your salary. Employers often sell some shares to cover this ("sell to cover").

**2. At sale: capital gains**
The difference between the sale price and the value at exercise/vesting is a **capital gain**, short- or long-term depending on how long you held (rules differ for foreign and unlisted shares; Module 12).

:::example
100 RSUs vest at ₹2,000 → **₹2,00,000 added to salary** (taxed at slab).
Sold two years later at ₹2,600 → **₹60,000 capital gain**.
:::

## Foreign employer shares: report them

If your shares are of a **foreign company** (e.g. a US-listed parent), you must disclose them in your Indian return's **foreign assets schedule** every year you hold them. That means ITR-2 or ITR-3, not ITR-1. Missing this can bring very large penalties under the black-money law, even if the tax was paid. Dividends and gains may also be taxed abroad; a foreign tax credit may apply. **This is where a CA is worth it.**

## Concentration: the real risk

Your salary, your bonus, your RSUs and possibly your ESPP all depend on **one company**. If it struggles, you can lose income and savings together.

A common approach: **sell most shares as they vest** (the tax is due anyway), invest the proceeds per your allocation (Module 14), and keep only a **capped** amount of employer stock.

:::trap
Startup ESOPs: until there's a listing or a company buyback, they usually **can't be sold**, and many startups never deliver either. Exercising can trigger tax on paper value you can't sell. Value them near **zero** in your plan; treat a payout as a bonus. Never take a big salary cut purely for ESOPs you can't evaluate.
:::
