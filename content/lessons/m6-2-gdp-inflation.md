:::hook
"GDP grows 7%!" and "Prices up 6%!" can be in the same newspaper. One makes the country sound rich, the other makes your grocery bill feel worse. **Both can be true at once.** Knowing what each number measures stops headlines from steering your money decisions.
:::

## GDP in plain words

**GDP (Gross Domestic Product)** is the value of all **final** goods and services produced in India in a period: haircuts, cars, software exports, rice, Netflix subscriptions, government hospitals.

By who spends it:

**GDP = C + I + G + (X − M)**

- **C**: household consumption (the biggest part in India)
- **I**: investment (factories, machines, houses, roads)
- **G**: government spending
- **X − M**: exports minus imports

MoSPI's National Statistics Office releases GDP estimates every quarter. Early estimates get **revised**, sometimes noticeably. In February 2026 it moved to a new **base year (2022-23)**, using newer data sources such as GST records.

## Real vs nominal

- **Nominal GDP growth**: includes price increases.
- **Real GDP growth**: removes inflation, which is the "7%" in headlines.

:::example
Nominal GDP up 10%, prices up 4% → real growth ≈ **5.8%**.

The same logic applies to you. Arjun's 6% raise with 5% inflation is a **real raise of about 1%**. His lifestyle can improve by 1%, not 6%.
:::

## Inflation: how it's measured

- **CPI (Consumer Price Index)**: the price of a basket of what households buy, from MoSPI every month. It's RBI's target measure. From February 2026, CPI uses a **2024 base year** with an updated basket that reflects how people spend today (more services, phones, eating out).
- **Headline inflation**: the full CPI, including food and fuel, which swing a lot.
- **Core inflation**: excludes food and fuel, so it shows the underlying trend.
- **WPI (Wholesale Price Index)**: prices at the wholesale level. It's useful, but it isn't what you pay.

:::checklive cpi
MoSPI's CPI page shows the latest monthly inflation. Look at "All India, Combined".
:::

## Jobs and incomes

GDP can grow while jobs grow slowly ("jobless growth"), or while gains go mostly to some people. Useful extra data: the **Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)** for jobs, and **per-capita income** for averages per person. No single number tells the whole story.

:::dive Recession, slowdown, stagflation
- **Slowdown**: growth falls but stays positive (e.g. 8% → 5%).
- **Recession**: the economy actually shrinks. A common rule of thumb is two quarters in a row of negative real growth. India's last was 2020, during COVID.
- **Stagflation**: low growth with high inflation, the hardest mix for a central bank, because fighting inflation hurts growth further.

For your money, slowdowns mean slower hiring and raises, and sometimes layoffs. That's exactly when an emergency fund and your own health insurance prove their worth.
:::

:::dive Why your "personal inflation" can be higher
CPI is a national average basket. If most of your spending is rent in Hyderabad, eating out and private healthcare, your costs may rise faster than CPI. That's why goal planning (Module 13) often uses a higher inflation rate for education and healthcare than headline CPI.
:::
