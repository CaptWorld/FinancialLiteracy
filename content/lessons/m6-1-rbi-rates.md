:::hook
Every couple of months, six people in Mumbai decide one number. Within weeks it changes the EMI on lakhs of home loans, the rate your bank offers on FDs, and the price of government bonds. **That number is the repo rate.** Understanding it turns "economy news" into "news about my money".
:::

## What RBI is trying to do

Since 2016, India has had **flexible inflation targeting**: RBI must aim for **4% CPI inflation**, with an allowed band of **2% to 6%**, while keeping growth in mind. The government renewed this target in 2026 for **2026–2031**.

The decision is made by the **Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)**: three members from RBI (including the Governor) and three outside experts. It meets about **every two months** and announces the **repo rate** and its "stance" (its signal about the next moves).

## How the repo rate works

The **repo rate** is what banks pay when they borrow short-term money from RBI against government bonds. It sets the floor for the cost of money across the economy.

| Situation | RBI tends to… | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation high or rising | **Raise** repo | Loans costlier, deposits pay more → less spending → inflation eases (with a lag of months) |
| Growth weak, inflation low | **Cut** repo | Loans cheaper, deposits pay less → more borrowing and spending → growth picks up |
| Balanced | **Hold** | Wait and watch |

RBI also manages **liquidity** (how much spare money banks have) with tools like CRR and market operations. That's the plumbing behind the headline.

The current repo rate and the latest policy statement are on RBI's home page.
:::

## How it reaches you

:::example
RBI cuts the repo rate by 0.5%.

- **Arjun's repo-linked home loan** (₹39.2 lakh left, 19 years): at the next reset, 8.5% → 8.0%. EMI could fall from ₹34,713 to about **₹33,500**, or the tenure shortens if he keeps the EMI.
- **His FD** booked at 7.25%: unchanged until maturity. A new FD next month might pay noticeably less.
- **Debt mutual funds and bonds** he might hold later: prices usually **rise** when rates fall (Lesson 5).
- **Stocks**: often cheer cheaper money, but it's one factor among many.
:::

**Winners and losers.** Rate cuts help borrowers and hurt savers who live on interest (often retirees). Rate hikes do the opposite. Neither is "good" in general.

:::dive Why it takes months: "transmission" and lags
A repo change doesn't instantly change everything. Repo-linked loans reset within about three months, but deposit rates adjust slowly as old FDs mature. Spending and prices respond over 6–18 months. That's why RBI tries to act on its **forecast** of inflation, not just today's number, and why critics often say it moved too late or too early. It's also why "RBI will cut next month" headlines are guesses.
:::

:::dive Real interest rates
What matters to savers and borrowers is the rate **after inflation** (Module 1). An FD at 7% with inflation at 6% earns about 1% real. RBI watches the **real policy rate** (repo minus expected inflation) to judge whether policy is tight or loose. When inflation falls but repo stays, policy actually gets tighter.
:::
